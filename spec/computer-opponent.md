# Computer opponent

Written by the main session at promotion, never by a research task. The summary describes only
`supported` and `corroborated` claims. Everything else stays in the table. The computer plays three
of the four seats in the unregistered build, so every claim here is about Pro's own AI as read from
its code; none of it is measured play (T28 corroborates), and none of it is a claim about playing
strength — what the code does, not whether it plays well.

## Summary

*Isle Wars Pro*, unregistered, as read from its code: the computer's turn uses the shared income,
dice and card rules (F011, F013, F014) and makes its own choices through four handlers — placement
by island ranks and candidate scores with drawn splits (C-AI-01, C-AI-02), attacks in drawn rounds
over gated candidate pairs picked uniformly from a same-island-weighted list (C-AI-03, C-AI-04),
one end-of-turn move from a hard-coded 23-country table with five corridor fallbacks and a 7-in-8
centre veto (C-AI-05), and card play by the fixed trade priority with at most one set a turn
(C-AI-06). A single rank table serves placement and attack weighting (C-AI-02). After the last
human seat is eliminated the attack rounds gain 4 while the seat holds at least 8 countries
(C-AI-07). The difficulty level changes nothing in the AI — no computer handler reads it; it only
guarantees the human seat a minimum of starting countries in the deal, 5/9/14/16 for levels 1 to 4
(C-AI-08). The quality of the play is unestablished (C-AI-09 keeps it that way).
