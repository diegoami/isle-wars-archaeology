## Answer

Every source below is `static:decompile` of `Islepro.exe`, sha256 `c4d7a248646454adeb48232e3ac7b3d2c0f08b290051a79d6fe6e47bb76b594e`; scripts are cited as in `questions/pro-static.md` (kind, number, name when it has one, handler). Nothing is quoted: the rules are described in our words (rule 18). "The human" is the code that runs for a seat marked human in the four-seat seat table (F013's reading); "the computer" is the code the computer-turn chain runs. Globals that recur: the 14-entry event table built once at movie start, each entry holding one of five event-kind numbers (two slots each for the earthquake, flood, rebellion and centre-move kinds, six for the centre-increase kind); the previous event's drawn slot number, reset to zero at the deal; the frequency setting (four values); the player-turn counter (F012's); the list of the three production-centre countries; the 46-entry army and ownership tables (F013's CV and CC); the four per-seat country lists; the seat table and its deal-time copy; the sound switch (sound plays when it is zero); and the speed setting that scales the busy-wait delays of every animation.

**In one paragraph.** Between one seat's turn and the next, one dispatch frame always draws a single random number whose range is set by the frequency option — 1 to 8, 1 to 14 or 1 to 21, and an unset bound when the option is set to its fourth value (the outcome is then forced) — and an event happens only on one fixed outcome of that draw. Four conditions force the non-event outcome after the draw: the first six player-turns (the counter must have reached 7), any seat holding more than 38 countries, a game already over, and the transition immediately after an event (the event path sets the counter back to 5, which holds the gate shut until it climbs to 7 again — see the harmonizing note in Observations on F012's contrary gloss). Before the event branch the same frame also counts human-seat flags and can route to the stop screen (no live human seat) or to the >32-country human concession and return, so a winning draw can be pre-empted; the draw, the gates and every effect are otherwise shared by all seats. When the event fires, a second draw picks one of fourteen table slots, redrawn once if it repeats the previous event's slot; the slot's kind then runs one of five routines: an earthquake message and a flood message both lead to the same handler, which picks a block of 3 or 4 countries by the armies standing on a window next to the block among four random candidates and replaces each block country's armies with a fresh new count (1 to half-minus-one of the old for counts above 6, 1 to old-minus-one for counts 1 to 6, floored at one; the struck block starts one country after the scored window's start, so country 1 is never struck); the rebellion needs at least two seats holding three or more countries, shuffles them, takes the first as victim and the second as receiver, draws a victim country from the victim's holdings (redrawing up to eight times to prefer a country with more than one army), and hands that one country to the receiver with its armies unchanged; the centre-increase kind doubles the armies standing on each of the three production-centre countries, capped at 999, whoever owns them; and the centre-move kind repaints the old centres' totals plain and re-places three fresh centres on a new uniform draw. The centres themselves are placed at every game start — after the deal, before the first-turn draw — as three distinct uniformly random countries (three fixed positions of a fresh shuffle), with no spacing or island rule, and nothing but the move event ever changes the list: no capture, rebellion, flood or rebuild touches it. No event effect handler tests whose turn it is or whether a seat is human or computer; the dispatch itself is pre-empted by the human concession (above). The only seat-path asymmetry found is that three computer handlers read the centre list as an input — placement candidates, a post-capture army-splitting branch, and a move cancellation — while no human-path handler reads it at all.

#### Q-HAZ-04 Is there an event that halves the armies of a province; what selects its victim (uniform choice, the largest, the leader's), and how is odd-numbered loss rounded?

- **As read (about 95%).** Yes: the flood — and the earthquake is the same event with a different message (Q-HAZ-05). Victim selection is stack-seeking, not uniform and not the leader's: the handler draws four random starting positions on the board, scores each by the sum of the armies standing on the three countries from that position (the start and the two after it), and takes the highest-scoring candidate; ties go to the later candidate. It then strikes the block of countries *after* the winning start — 3 or 4 of them (a fresh draw of one or the other), the countries start+1 through start+f; the scored window is start through start+2, so the block's last country is one past the window's end when 3 are struck and two past it when 4 are, as read from the decompiled text and confirmed in the bytecode listing. The start is drawn from 1 to 46 minus the block size, so the struck set always lies inside the board and **country 1 is never struck**. The effect per struck country: from the stored count, if it is more than 6 the new ceiling is half of it (integer division, rounding down), otherwise the count itself; one is then subtracted; a result below one is raised to one; and the country's **new** army count is a fresh uniform draw from 1 up to that ceiling. So the bound is on the replacement, not on the damage: a count of 20 finishes in 1 to 9 (a loss of 11 to 19), one of 100 in 1 to 49; small counts of 1 to 6 finish in 1 to count-minus-one (a six-army country can end at five, a lone army stays 1). The owner is never changed and the countries keep their identity; the handler has no ownership or eligibility test of any kind — any country but country 1 can be struck, and the animation flashes the block three times before applying the damage.

### The centre rules (for later tasks and for malpaco)

- Count: always 3, on the one board; no player-count or board-size dependence.
- Start: three distinct uniformly random countries each game (three fixed positions of a fresh shuffle), drawn after the deal, before the first turn; no spacing rule.
- Move: only the centre-move event, all three at once, to a fresh uniform draw; no adjacency, distance or water constraint.
- Effect: none per turn — no income term (F013), no placement term for the human; the increase event doubles the stored armies on all three, cap 999.
- Capture/destroy: impossible by gameplay; the list has exactly one writer (the placement routine); ownership changes leave the centre with the country; the marker colour persists (no capture-path repaint found) until a move event or a new game.
- Readers: the event frame (dispatch and move branch), the two centre routines, and three computer handlers (placement candidates, post-capture allocation, move cancellation); no human-path reader exists.
- Marks: the centre colour on each centre's army-total text (set at placement and at each increase), the plain colour restored by the move event; a panel caption names the production centres at game start.

**A contradiction with F012's gloss, stated not smoothed over.** F012 (Q-TRN-04, Q-VIC-04) describes the counter-reset frame as lowering the count "so that another event can follow soon after". The arithmetic of the two handlers as read here says the opposite: the gate needs a count of at least 7, the counter only ever grows by one per player-turn, and the event path pins it to 5 — so the reset *guarantees* that the transition immediately after an event cannot fire another one, and the earliest possible repeat is the second transition after. Without the reset, a late-game event (count in the dozens) would leave the gate wide open on the very next transition. The code facts F012 states (the frame sets the counter to 5; the frame is on the event path) are confirmed here; only the purpose gloss differs, and on F012's own terms — the reset is a cooldown, not a speedup. Nothing in F015's answers depends on which gloss is right; F012's sentence should be amended by whoever amends findings.

### Where random numbers are drawn (Randomness)

- **The event draw:** one uniform draw per transition over 8, 14 or 21 by the setting (the fourth setting's draw has an unset bound; the outcome is overridden regardless); the generator and its distribution belong to Director's runtime (T18).
- **The kind draw:** one uniform draw over the 14 slots per event, redrawn once when it repeats the previous event's slot (the redraw can still repeat it); long-run slot shares are uniform, so about 3 in 7 events are centre increases and about 1 in 7 each of the others.
- **The flood:** the block size (one draw), the four candidate starts (four draws), and each struck country's new army count (one draw per struck country) — eight draws when three countries are struck and nine when four are, besides the slot.
- **The rebellion:** the seat shuffle (a draw-and-remove permutation over the eligible seats) and up to eight draws for the victim country.
- **The centres:** one fresh 46-country permutation per placement call (game start and each move event).
- The battle, card and deal draws are F011's, T22's and T18's.

## Inferences

- **The events are a mild pro-growth force, not a rubber band.** The helping kinds hold 8 of 14 slots, the harm kinds' replacement never exceeds half-minus-one of the old count (old-minus-one for counts 1–6, floor one) and is seat-blind, and the rebellion is drawn by lot: nothing drags the leader down. A malpaco that wants a rubber band must add one — Pro does not have it. From Q-HAZ-06, Q-HAZ-08, Q-HAZ-04.
- **The centres are lottery prizes, not economy.** Three random countries, re-drawn whenever the centre-move event fires (how many times a game is a play-rate question for T28, not a code fact), worth one doubling each time the increase fires: their value is the timing coincidence of holding armies there when the increase lands, plus the computer's centre-aware placement, allocation and move handling (T27). A design that pays centres per turn (malpaco's draft) is a different game — stronger and steadier. From Q-CEN-02, Q-CEN-03, Q-HAZ-08.
- **The event system cannot break a locked board open, but it can trim it.** The 39-country gate switches events off exactly when a board locks; below it, floods shave the biggest stacks and rebellions move single countries — enough to keep counts moving, never to eliminate. From Q-HAZ-03, Q-HAZ-04, Q-HAZ-07, and F012's stalemate cluster.
- **The rebellion's message can lie.** The event frame types the rebellion message before the rebellion handler runs, and the handler exits without acting when fewer than two seats hold three or more countries — so a game in its endgame can announce rebellions that change nothing. T28 should count announced-but-empty events. From Q-HAZ-06.
- **The flood's offset is a likely authoring slip worth keeping an eye on.** The struck block begins one country after the scored window's start (and ends one country past the window's end when 3 are struck, two past when 4 are); the effect is that the flood sometimes damages a country that played no part in the targeting and spares the window's first country, and country 1 can never be struck. A faithful port should decide whether to reproduce the offset or target the window itself. From Q-HAZ-04.

## Alternatives considered

- **The counter reset might speed events up, as F012's gloss says.** Excluded by the arithmetic: the gate needs 7, the counter grows once per player-turn, and 5 is below 7 — the two transitions after a reset cannot both pass; at most the second can. The reset's only work is to guarantee the gap. (See the harmonizing note.)
- **The fourth setting might not be "never".** The button images say Never (cast member names), the setting exists only to suppress, and the override forces the non-event outcome; the unset draw bound is a code smell, not a second behaviour. About 80% that the setting is clean.
- **The flood's struck block might be a decompiler misrender of the window.** Checked in the bytecode listing: the struck indices are start-plus-one through start-plus-size, distinct from the window's start through start-plus-two. Both index expressions are as decompiled.
- **The rebellion's victim draw might be leader-weighted through the shuffle.** The shuffle is a uniform draw-and-remove permutation over the eligible list; its first element is uniform over eligible seats. No weighting exists in the handler.
- **A centre country might lose its mark when captured** (the repaint living in the score's member swaps rather than in any script). Not excluded: the scripts set the text member's colour, and the capture paths change the map sprites — whether the total's colour survives a capture on screen is T24's. The list itself is untouched (searched).
- **The event table might hold a sixth kind in an unused slot.** Excluded: all fourteen slots hold values, and the event frame's branches cover every distinct value present.

## What this does not establish

- **That the decompiler is faithful in every handler.** One decompiler (ProjectorRays 0.2.0); bytecode comparisons were made for the flood's arithmetic and offsets, the dispatch's gates and ranges, the rebellion's constants and store, and the event frame's draws, as Method lists; no run corroborates any behaviour.
- **Runtime behaviour and the score.** The frame order — including the counter-reset frame's exact position on the event path — and which sprite carries which button are not decoded: the chains here rest on the jumps each handler makes, read one by one, as in F012 and F013. The messages are the text fields the scripts set, not observed screens; the animations' felt timing is the busy-wait helpers' and the speed setting's (T24).
- **The registered build,** and everything gated by the registration global (the frequency option's unlock, the seat toggles) or by the upgrade check: the refusals are read from the unregistered build's own code; what registration really unlocks is `unknown` (rule 19).
- **The played frequencies.** Every rate above is a draw range, a slot count or a gate in the code; what players actually see per minute (T28 counts them) depends on the runtime generator, the turn length and the gates' incidence. The code's draw weights are not the played rates.
- **The computer's reactions** to centres and events (the weights behind the three centre-list reads, and how the AI adapts to event damage): T27's.
- **The deal, the income formula, combat and cards** that share these handlers' frames (T18, F013, F011, T22): here only the event and centre claims are made — including, from F013, only the absence of a centre term in income.
- **DOS Isle Wars.** Nothing here is claimed for it (rule 19).
