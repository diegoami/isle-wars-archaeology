## Answer

Every source below is `static:decompile` of `Islepro.exe`, sha256 `c4d7a248646454adeb48232e3ac7b3d2c0f08b290051a79d6fe6e47bb76b594e`; scripts are cited as in `questions/pro-static.md` (kind, number, name when it has one, handler). Nothing is quoted: the rules are described in our words (rule 18). "The human" is the code that runs when a person clicks or is asked; "the computer" is the code the computer-turn handler runs for a computer seat. Four globals recur: the seat number whose turn it is, a count of player-turns since the deal, a flag that the concession offer has been refused, and a flag that the game is over (set to the winning seat's number, and used only as "over or not" by every reader but one — the end screens re-derive the winner from the country counts).

**In one paragraph.** Seats are numbered 1 to 4 by colour; each turn the number advances by one and wraps, and seats with no countries are skipped, so the order is fixed and only loses members. The first seat of the game is chosen by an animated marker that settles on a randomly drawn seat, and the stored number is then stepped back one so that the turn-advance step lands on the seat shown. A turn is: income computed for the seat, then (human) placement, free-form attack and one move ended by Pass, move or a failed attack (F011), or (computer) the whole turn inside one handler. Between turns a frame draws one event chance (suppressed for the first six player-turns, and on any transition at which some seat holds 39 or more countries — a test of the present counts, not a switch that stays off), offers the concession, and dispatches to the next turn. The game is over when a seat's country count reaches 46, but where that is caught differs: the human's turn screen tests the counts every frame, so a human conquest ends the turn at once; the computer's battle chain sets no game-over flag, so after a computer captures the last country its own move and card draw still run, and the conquest is caught at the next turn advance (the steps between are gated on the flag, which they only read). The game is also over when a human seat holds 33 or more countries, the only human seat, with the game not over and the offer not yet refused: the computers then concede collectively, the accept button ends the game at once as a win, and the refuse button returns to play with the offer banned for the rest of the game. Nothing else ends a game: no turn, round or time limit exists, no share, objective, income, survival or named-player win exists, and no drawn result exists (when the scores screen must name a winner without a 46-country conqueror it takes the seat with the most countries, ties going to the lower seat number). A seat is eliminated at zero countries; its cards are wiped, not inherited (at a later rebuild of the seat lists than the one that marks it out, not necessarily at the capture). Leaving mid-game through the new-game button with 1 to 9 countries credits the human with a loss for the blue, yellow and red seats; the green seat's branch stores its weak-position verdict into a local instead of the quit-early global — read as a slip in the original's code, one name off — so a green human leaving at 1 to 9 countries with the flag clear has nothing recorded, while a flag already set still costs the loss. With 10 or more countries, or at zero, nothing is recorded only when the quit-early flag is still clear — the elimination prompt sets that flag and its play-out button keeps it, so a human who chose the play-out and then leaves is sent to the scores screen and recorded a loss. The only persistent record is six counters — the human seat's wins and losses at each of three difficulty levels — written, obfuscated, to a file beside the game; unless the registration flag is set, a result is written only when the counters sum to 50 or fewer before it, so the file reaches at most 51 games.

### Turn sequence

#### Q-TRN-01 Which phases make up a turn, in what order, and can a phase be skipped, repeated or done out of order?

#### What the end records (`writescores`)

- **As read (about 90%).** One file, beside the game's own movie, holds six counters: the human seat's wins and losses at each of three difficulty levels. ### Surrender

#### Q-SUR-01 Does a game end through an offer to concede before the last opponent is eliminated; who makes the offer and to whom?

- **As read (about 90%).** Yes. The computers make one collective offer to the human: in the between-turns frame, when the game is not over, the offer has not been refused, exactly one human seat is in the game, and that human seat holds more than 32 countries (33 or more) — a computer holding that many cancels the offer — the game branches to a concession screen instead of the next turn. The screen's message says the other forces concede the game to the leading army (with 33 or more of 46 countries the leader is necessarily the human seat). It is one offer from all the computer seats at once, not one per computer. `Islepro.txt`'s end-of-game fragment ("concede that game and play another one", `knowledge-map.md`, Surrender) agrees that a concession ends a game without naming its trigger, which the code adds.
- **Same rule?** The offer exists only on the human's side; no code path offers a concession to a dominant computer, and a computer never concedes on its own.
- **Sources:** `static:decompile` `Islepro.exe` sha256 `c4d7a248646454adeb48232e3ac7b3d2c0f08b290051a79d6fe6e47bb76b594e`; `BehaviorScript 4` / `exitFrame()` (the trigger and its four conditions); `BehaviorScript 465` / `exitFrame()` (the screen and its message); `BehaviorScript 469` / `exitFrame()` (the screen's two buttons).

#### Q-SUR-04 How does the offer work: whose choices, and what does each choice cost?

- **As read (about 90%).** The human chooses between two buttons. Accept ends the game at once: the frame chain goes to the win screen, whose message names the human's army the winner (its threshold test is passed by 33 countries), and the game is scored as a win for the human seat. Refuse returns to play — and sets the refusal flag, which no handler clears for the rest of the game (only the new-game reset clears it; the deal handler does not touch it): the offer never returns, in that game, whatever the count becomes. The cost of refusing is therefore the loss of the ending itself: from then on the game can end only at 46 countries or by leaving (Q-SUR-05); nothing else is charged — no armies, cards or income. A side effect read as written: the elimination prompt's "play it out" button sets the same flag, so a human who lets the computers play on after elimination has also banned any later concession offer (unreachable in practice, an eliminated seat holding 0 countries; it matters only with several human seats, registered).
- **Same rule?** Not applicable (the human's choice).
- **Sources:** `static:decompile` `Islepro.exe` sha256 `c4d7a248646454adeb48232e3ac7b3d2c0f08b290051a79d6fe6e47bb76b594e`; `CastScript 609 ACCEPT2` / `mouseUp()` (accept: to the win screen); `CastScript 615 KEEP3` / `mouseUp()` (refuse: the flag, back to play); `BehaviorScript 463` / `exitFrame()` (the win screen after accepting); `MovieScript 23 Newgame` / `Newgame()` (the only clear of the flag; `MovieScript 5 Putinboard` / `Putinboard()`, the deal, clears the quit-early and stop flags but not this one); `CastScript 611 KEEP2` / `mouseUp()` (the flag set from the elimination prompt).

#### Q-SUR-05 Can a human resign mid-game, and what happens to their countries, armies and the game?

- **As read (about 80% for the new-game route, 65% for the stop route; the green-seat exception below about 90% as a mechanical reading of the store, and about 80% that it is a slip rather than a deliberate exception).** There is no resign control on the board; the ways out are the new-game menu button and, when the human seat is eliminated, the elimination prompt. The new-game button: with exactly one human seat and the game under way, if the human seat holds 1 to 9 countries (alive but weak) the click credits the human with a loss — a message says so, the loss is recorded through the quit-early global — and the scores screen follows; but that holds for the blue, yellow and red seats only. The green seat's branch, alone of the four colour branches, stores its weak-position verdict into a local variable instead of the quit-early global — the decompiled text shows an assignment to a name that is not among the handler's declared globals, and the bytecode listing confirms a local store where the other three branches store the global — while the loss message, the jump to the scores screen and the remaining tests all read the global. So a green human leaving with 1 to 9 countries and a clear global goes to the options screen and nothing is recorded; if the global was already set, the loss path still applies to the green seat as to the others. This is read as a slip in the original game's code — one name that does not match the declared global, so the store lands on an undeclared local — not as a deliberate green-seat exception, and no other answer in this finding changes. With 10 or more countries, or at zero, no branch sets anything; what follows depends on the quit-early flag as it already stands. If it is clear, nothing is recorded and the options screen for a new game follows. If it is set — the elimination prompt sets it whenever it is shown, the play-out button leaves it set, and only the deal and the reset after the stop screen clear it — the same loss message and the scores screen follow, and the scores screen records a loss (subject to the record's cap). So a human who was eliminated, chose the play-out and then clicks new game is recorded a loss; nothing is recorded at zero only if the flag is clear, that is, only if the elimination prompt has not been shown since the flag was last cleared. In every case the seat's countries and armies are simply abandoned: nothing redistributes them and the game does not continue (the board is left as it stands; the game is over for that run). The elimination prompt (shown when the human seat's count reaches zero): one button accepts the loss and goes to the scores screen (with one human seat; with several it goes on to the frame after), the other lets the computer armies play the game out, the human watching; during that play-out the between-turns frame's stop route is bypassed by the prompt's own flag. The stop route itself: when no live human seat remains and no flag is set, the between-turns frame branches to a stop screen instead of the next turn (so an all-computer game does not run forever unwatched), whose buttons lead to the scores screen or on; which exact combinations reach it is the least certain reading here (the frames are identified by what they do, not by the score).

## Method

- **Artifact and tools.** The decompiled scripts of F010's run `pro-r1` (ProjectorRays 0.2.0 on the movie of `Islepro.exe`, sha256 above), read in place on the owner's machine in the folder `$IWA_STATIC` names; nothing was copied into the repository (rule 18). The scripts' hashes (`ls_sha256`) are those of `experiments/E008-static-triage/measurements/iwp-extract-pro-r1.json`; E010 lists the scripts read with them, and `experiments/E010-pro-turn-victory-reading/check_cited_scripts.py` checks that this finding cites no script, name or handler the record does not have, and (with `--disk`) that each listed script on disk, hashed in place, has the record's hash.
- **What was read.** In full: each handler cited above (the turn advance, the first-turn draw, the between-turns dispatch, the turn-start chain, the turn screen's end tests, the end and scores screens, the concession and elimination screens and all their buttons, the leave button, the info rebuild, the deal's and new game's resets, the income handler's game-over test, the computer placement handler's, the computer's battle chain from the attack handler to the capture, the human's two capture frames, the failed-attack display, the two score-file handlers). No bytecode comparison was made for this finding's handlers except one: the green-seat branch of the leave button (Q-SUR-05), where the answer turns on a store's target rather than on a comparison, was checked against the script's bytecode listing — the three non-green seats' branches store to the quit-early global and the green branch to a local, and the later tests read the global. None of the other answers turns on a comparison operator or a constant below the granularity the decompiled text states plainly (the one number that could be misread, the concession boundary, appears as a single strict comparison in a short handler).
- **What was searched, in the terminal only and with nothing kept.** Every place that sets or clears the game-over global (four handlers, listed in Q-VIC-07); every user of the player-turn counter (two); every user of the concession-refusal, quit-early, stop and do-not-score globals; the callers of the first-turn draw; and every jump to the between-turn, begin, start, event, concede, game-over and scores frames.
- **What was not read.** The score (frame order and which sprite carries which button), the cast members (pictures, texts, sounds), the obfuscation routine behind the score file, and the 61 scripts that no cast member points at (F010). Where the answer needs the frame order it says so.
- **Static first (rule 24).** This finding is the static answer; no run was made. T28 corroborates from recorded play, with the counts above.

## Observations

### The country-count thresholds, side by side

| Count | What the code does there | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | the seat is eliminated: live mark cleared at the capture's rebuild, cards wiped at a later rebuild (at the latest the next turn advance), seat skipped | turn advance, info rebuild |
| 1 to 9 | leaving mid-game credits the human with a loss (blue, yellow and red seats; the green seat's branch stores into a local, not the quit-early global, so no loss unless that flag is already set) | the leave button |
| 10 or more, or 0 (and 1 to 9 for the green seat) | leaving mid-game records nothing, if the quit-early flag is clear (after the elimination prompt it is set, and a loss is recorded, whatever the count) | the leave button |
| more than 30 (31+) | the win screen's winner test; below it the scores screen falls back to the maximum | the two end screens |
| more than 32 (33+) | the computers' collective concession offer, human seat only, once per game | the between-turns frame |
| more than 38 (39+) | no event is drawn at a transition while any seat holds this many (re-tested each transition; not latched) | the between-turns frame |
| 46 | the game is over: on the next frame for a human conquest; at the next turn advance for a computer's, after its move and card draw | the human's turn screen, the turn advance, the income and computer placement handlers |

### The two paths side by side

| | Human | Computer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Turn | frames, with the conquest test every frame | one handler chain whose steps only read the game-over flag; its own conquest is caught at the next turn advance |
| Can resign | by leaving: a loss at 1 to 9 countries for the blue, yellow and red seats (the green seat's branch misses the flag: nothing recorded unless it is already set); nothing at 10 or more or at 0 unless the quit-early flag is already set | no resign path; the collective concession only |

### Where random numbers are drawn (Randomness)

- **The first-turn draw:** a number of steps of 9 to 18 (a draw of 1 to 10 plus 8), and per step a seat of 1 to 4, redrawn up to twice when it repeats the previous step. Cosmetic: the compensation of Q-TRN-02 makes the shown seat play first either way; the redraws change only the animation.
- **The event gate:** one draw of 1 to 8, 1 to 14 or 1 to 21 by the frequency setting, the event on one outcome; never when the setting is "never". The generator and its distribution belong to Director's runtime (T18).
- No other draw exists on the paths this finding covers (the battle and AI draws are F011's and T27's).

### Agreement with the documents

`Islepro.txt` (knowledge-map.md, Turn sequence and Surrender rows) says the game will "randomly decide who will take the first turn" (agrees: the draw of Q-TRN-02) and offers, at the end of game, to "concede that game and play another one" (agrees in kind: the concession screen of Q-SUR-01; the fragment does not say when, or that the computers offer collectively, which the code adds). The share "half the provinces and half the armies" that malpaco's `RULES.md` guesses for the trigger matches neither half: the code reads one measure, the human seat's country count, at 33 of 46 (about 72%), and no army share at all.

## Inferences

- The concession is the design answer to the stalemate cluster, and it is narrow: it fires only for a dominant human, only once, and refusing it removes the only ending short of conquest. A faithful model that keeps the offer should decide whether refusal is final (as read) or repeating (malpaco's choice to make). From Q-SUR-03 and Q-SUR-04.
- For an all-computer observation game the code has no ending at all until 46 countries: the stop route needs the watcher's flags. T28's all-AI games should watch for this. From Q-VIC-01 and Q-SUR-05.
- The win screens' re-derivation of the winner from the counts makes the game-over global's seat number load-bearing nowhere visible; the swapped red and green numbers in the computer placement handler's test are therefore latent, not active. From Q-VIC-07.

## Alternatives considered

- **The first-turn compensation is a bug and the seat before the shown one starts.** Not excluded (the score is not decoded); the reading that the author stepped the number back exactly to cancel the advance step's increment is the simpler one, and every turn after the first passes through the advance anyway. About 80%.
- **A limit or alternative win condition hides in the 61 unwritten scripts.** Not excluded (F010); the searches covered the 213 written scripts, and every frame on the game's own loop is written.
- **The stop route is the elimination prompt's accept path.** The accept button's handler was read: it goes to the scores screen or the frame after, not through the stop frame; the stop route is a separate branch of the between-turns frame. The two frames' relationship remains score-dependent, hence the 65%.
- **"More than 32" is "32 or more".** The comparison is strict in a short handler; at 32 no offer, at 33 the offer. Read as written.

## What this does not establish

- **That the decompiler is faithful in every handler.** One decompiler (ProjectorRays 0.2.0); one bytecode comparison was made, for the green-seat store of Q-SUR-05 (Method says why), and no run corroborates a behaviour.
- **Runtime behaviour:** the frame order (the score), which sprite carries which button, the screens' look and sounds. Every "the frame chain goes to" here rests on the jumps each script makes, read one by one.
- **The registered build,** and everything gated by the registration global (the score cap of `writescores`, the seat toggles, the other gates F011 lists). What registration unlocks is `unknown` (rule 19).
- **The score file's obfuscation** (the routine was not read) and its portability.
- **The events, cards, bombs and computer choices** that share these handlers' frames (T23, T22, T27); here only the turn, victory and concession claims are made.
- **DOS Isle Wars.** Nothing here is claimed for it (rule 19).
- **How often games stall,** or any rate: every number above is a threshold or a count in the code, not an observed frequency (T28).
