## Answer

Every source below is `static:decompile` of `Islepro.exe`, sha256 `c4d7a248646454adeb48232e3ac7b3d2c0f08b290051a79d6fe6e47bb76b594e`; scripts are cited as in `questions/pro-static.md` (kind, number, name when it has one, handler). Nothing is quoted: the rules are described in our words (rule 18). "The human" is the code that runs for a seat marked human in the four-seat opponent table; "the computer" is the code that runs for a seat marked computer (the unregistered build's default is one human, three computers). Globals that recur: the seat whose turn it is; the four per-seat card hands, each a five-slot list in which zero marks an empty slot; the fixed 34-entry card list set once at movie start (the deck); the flag that the seat captured at least one country this turn (set by either dice handler on a capture, cleared at every turn start, by the failed-attack frame and by two card-screen `exitFrame` handlers, Q-BON-05); the match flag of the human's turn-start check; the computer's chosen-match and bonus pair; and the pool of armies left to place and the earned number of this turn (F013).

**In one paragraph.** Every seat starts every game with an empty hand. The only way a card enters a hand is the draw after a capturing turn: exactly one card per turn in which the seat captured at least one country, for the human (drawn on a screen of its own at the end of its turn) and the computer (drawn silently inside its turn handler after its move) alike — two captures and five captures earn the same one card, and a turn whose attack failed earns none. The draw picks uniformly among the entries of a fixed list of 34 cards that was written in once at movie start and is never shuffled, never depleted and never refuses a draw: the deck is a weighted table, not a pile. The kinds are numbered 1 to 8: three bomb-match cards, two anti-bombs, eight +5 cards, six +8 cards, three +15 cards, six wildcards, five doubling cards, and one bomb. The bomb, the list's last entry, is special twice over: a hand with no cards cannot draw it (the empty-hand draw reads one entry short), and a hand that draws it loses every card in it at once. A hand holds five cards. A human capturing with a full hand is sent to a discard screen and must click one card away before the draw runs; a computer in the same position discards by a fixed priority — its anti-bomb first, then its bomb-match card, then a +5 card, else a random slot. At the top of the turn, after income and before placement, both seats' hands are checked for a match: three cards of one tradeable kind (the bomb-match kind, the +5 kind, the +8 kind, the +15 kind or the doubling kind), with up to two wildcards counted toward the kind — three wildcards alone are no match. The human with a match is routed through a trade-in screen: pick three cards, trade (the set is consumed and 5 or 8 or 15 armies join the pool, or the pool doubles, or — for the bomb-match kind — nothing is added and the flow detours to a bombing click), or skip. The computer trades automatically, at most one set, by a fixed priority (15, then 8, then doubling, then 5, then the bomb), and its placement handler applies the add. The bomb halves: the bombed country's armies are replaced by a uniform draw of 1 to half of what stood there, ownership untouched — no capture, no pool change. If the victim's owner holds an anti-bomb card, nothing happens to the armies and the anti-bomb is consumed — the computer's branch compacts the owner's hand, while the human's removal leaves the owner's slot empty and its compaction call acts on the bomber's own hand (Q-BON-09).

### The hand limit

#### Q-BON-06 What is the hand limit, and what happens to a card drawn when the hand is full?

- **As read (human, about 90%).** Five. The hand is a five-slot list; the draw places into the first empty slot. When the human's draw screen is reached with all five slots full, its frame script counts the hand, announces that a card must be discarded by clicking, and routes to a discard screen that loops until a card is clicked away: each of the seven card-face sprites carries an identical click handler whose guard accepts a click only while the full-hand discard state stands (every other click exits at its first line), and that first accepted click clears the state, empties the clicked slot and shows the empty image. The flow then passes to the draw frame, which draws into the freed slot. The net effect of a capture with a full hand is therefore: discard one chosen card, draw one — the hand does not stay at five with the new card refused, and it does not grow past five. The draw handler itself has no full-hand branch: it is only ever reached with a free slot, by the discard detour; if the score ordered the frames otherwise the draw would write at an index no list has (a runtime error, not a rule) — the frame order is the score's, which is why this answer is 90% and not higher.
- **As read (computer, about 95%).** Five too, but the computer never draws into a full hand: its draw handler counts the hand first, and when all five slots are full it empties one slot by a fixed priority — the first anti-bomb card (kind 2) it holds, else the first bomb-match card (kind 1), else the first +5 card (kind 3), else a random slot — and then draws. The discarded card is gone (no discard pile exists to return to). So the computer's capture with a full hand is also discard one, draw one, with the choice made for it, and the choice throws away first exactly the two cards whose value is defensive or match-making (the anti-bomb, the bomb-match card) before any of the army cards.
- **Same rule?** The limit is the same (five); the enforcement differs (a forced human click on a card of the player's choice; an automatic priority that prefers shedding the anti-bomb, then the bomb-match card, then the +5 card).

### Matches and trade-ins

#### Q-BON-07 How many cards may be played in one turn, what makes a match (wildcards and their cap), and how often can a trade-in happen?

- **As read (the match and the adds about 95%; the once-per-turn timing about 90% for the human, 95% for the computer).** At most one trade-in per turn, and a trade-in is always exactly three cards. A match is three cards of one tradeable kind, with wildcards: the check counts the wildcards (kind 6) in the hand or in the picked three, caps that count at two, adds the capped count to each tradeable kind's count (kinds 1, 3, 4, 5 and 8), and a kind whose total passes two is the match. Two wildcards plus one card of a kind is therefore a match of that kind; one wildcard plus two cards likewise; three wildcards alone are no match of any kind (the cap holds every kind at two). The cap governs **detection** only; it does not bound what a match then removes, and the computer's removal step below can consume more wildcards than the cap allowed to detect the set. The anti-bomb (kind 2) counts toward no kind and cannot be picked; the bomb (kind 7) can never be picked because it never sits in a hand (Q-BON-04). The tradeable kinds and what a set adds (agreeing with F013's 5, 8, 15, doubling and bomb): kind 3 pays 5, kind 4 pays 8, kind 5 pays 15, kind 8 doubles the whole pool (the earned number is added to itself, and the earned number at that moment is the full income), kind 1 is the bomb match: it adds nothing — its sentinel value fails the add gate — and routes to the bombing click (Q-BON-08).
  - **Human.** The turn-start frame checks the hand after income: with a match it routes to the trade-in screen instead of placement, without it straight to placement. The screen shows the hand as five pickable cards; clicking toggles a pick (empty slots and anti-bombs refuse the click; once three are picked a fourth click does nothing until one is unpicked), and three picks arm the trade-in button. The trade-in button re-derives the kind from the picked three with the same wildcard rule: a valid set is consumed (the three slots are emptied and the hand compacted, cards sliding to the front), the add lands in the pool at once, and the flow returns to placement; the bomb match consumes the three and goes to the bombing click; an invalid pick (three cards with no kind at three) is announced with a sound, adds nothing, consumes nothing, and still continues to placement. A second button skips the trade-in entirely (nothing added, nothing consumed, straight to placement). Both exits and the bomb's return lead to placement, and the only jump to the trade-in screen found in the 213 decompiled scripts is the turn-start frame's — so as read the trade-in happens at most once a turn, always at the top, after income and before placement. A path through the score that re-entered the screen cannot be excluded (frame order not decoded), which is why the timing figure is 90%.
  - **Computer.** The same check runs in the turn-start frame, but instead of a screen the handler itself picks at most one set by a fixed priority and removes the cards at once: the +15 kind (kind 5) first, then the +8 kind (kind 4), then the doubling kind (kind 8), then the +5 kind (kind 3), then the bomb match (kind 1) — F013's priority, here with the kind numbers; the removal walks the hand in slot order and zeroes the first three cards that are the chosen kind or a wildcard, without a separate count for wildcards. Detection caps wildcards at two, but this removal does not: a hand ordered as three wildcards followed by one +15 card and an empty slot detects a +15 match (one kind-5 card plus the capped two) and the loop consumes all three wildcards, keeps the +15 card and pays 15. That is a computer-only corner: the human removes exactly the three cards it picked, and an all-wildcard pick is refused before any removal (below). The chosen set's add is stored and applied by the placement handler at the head of its work (added to the pool, or the pool doubled for kind 8, or the bombing branch for kind 1), announced in a status message. `Islepro.txt` agrees that a match is three of the same and that sets are exchanged at the top of the turn; the fragment does not mention wildcards, the cap, the values or the once-per-turn limit, which the code settles.
- **Same rule?** The match definition (detection), the wildcard cap on detection, the adds and the one-set-per-turn limit are shared. The **consumption** is not: the human removes exactly the three cards it picked, while the computer removes the first three slots holding the chosen kind or a wildcard and so can eat more wildcards than detection counted (the corner above). ### The bomb

#### Q-BON-08 Is there a card or ability that removes armies from an enemy province without an attack; how many, and from which provinces?

- **As read (about 90%).** Yes: the bomb match (three of kind 1, wildcards allowed) arms a bombing click for the human, or an automatic bombing for the computer. The effect, identical in both branches: the target country's armies are replaced by a uniform draw of 1 to half of what stood there — the code takes the army count, halves it with integer division, raises a zero to one, draws uniformly in 1 to that half, and writes the drawn number as the country's new army count. A country of 1 stays at 1; a country of 2 becomes 1; a country of 10 becomes 1 to 5 (about 3 on average); a country of 40 becomes 1 to 20. Ownership does not change, no army moves, nothing is captured, and the pool and the income are untouched (the bombing branch adds nothing — F013's sentinel — and the return frame goes to placement with the pool as it was). malpaco's guess of "remove 2 armies" is wrong for Pro: Pro redraws at half. The help text captured on the unregistered build agrees in kind — bombing any country diminishes its troops (F003) — and adds "any country": the human's click is refused only on the bomber's own countries (the refusal leaves the bombing state armed, so the player must click again); there is no adjacency requirement, any enemy country on the board may be picked.
  - **Human.** The trade-in's bomb match routes to a frame that arms the bombing-click state and shows a prompt; the board click handler, in that state, consumes the click on the first enemy country and applies the halving draw (with the anti-bomb check of Q-BON-09 first); a short frame script afterwards clears the state and returns the flow to placement.
  - **Computer.** Its placement handler, for the bomb match, builds two parallel lists over the enemy countries that border the computer's own countries (the same front-line notion as its attacks, F011): the candidate country numbers and, in the same order, those countries' army counts. It makes nine random draws over the candidate list. Each sample's **army count is then used as an index into the board's army table** for the comparison — the test reads the armies of the country whose number equals the sampled candidate's army count — while the sampled candidate's army count is stored as the comparison threshold, and the sampled candidate country is the one kept as the target. This is not a largest-of-nine-stack selection: the nine samples never compare the candidates' own stacks against each other, and a sampled army count above 46 (the board's country count) indexes past the army table's end. What the comparison then does under Director — which victim the nine samples actually leave, and how a void comparison behaves — is a runtime consequence this static reading does not establish. The adjacent bytecode confirms the extra army-table lookup (a loaded sample's count pushed as the index into the army table). If the computer holds no front-line enemy country the list is empty and the branch would read a list at position zero: a corner this static reading cannot resolve (Director's runtime behaviour on the empty list; unreachable while the bomber borders any enemy). The halving draw and the anti-bomb check are the human's.
- **Same rule?** The effect and the protection are shared; the target differs (any enemy country by the human's click; a drawn front-line country for the computer).

### The anti-bomb

#### Q-BON-09 Is there a card or ability that protects a province for a time; against what, and for how long?

- **As read (about 95% for the protection; the "nothing else reads it" absence about 85%).** Yes: the anti-bomb (kind 2, two in the deck). It protects not a province but its owner: when a bomb (the human's or the computer's) picks a country, the branches look at the victim country's owner and search that owner's hand for an anti-bomb card. If one is there, nothing happens to the armies — the branch announces the protection in a message with its own sound — and the anti-bomb is consumed, but the two branches consume it differently. The computer's branch empties the owner's anti-bomb slot and compacts the owner's (the victim's) hand in place. The human's branch empties the victim owner's anti-bomb slot and then calls the shared reorder-and-compaction handler — which operates on the seat whose turn it is, the **bomber**, and on the trade-in's still-marked picks, not on the victim. So in the human's branch the victim's hand is left with the emptied slot uncompacted, and the bomber's own hand is compacted a second time with the stale picks: a bomber hand of three bomb-match cards followed by, say, a +15 and a doubling card, with the first three picked, compacts to the two retained cards when the bomb match is traded, and the reorder call after the anti-bomb is found can clear those two retained cards at the still-marked first positions. The panel counts are refreshed in both. Without the card, the halving draw runs (Q-BON-08). The protection is instantaneous and per bomb: it does not expire, it is not tied to the bombed country, and it is spent by the first bomb that arrives, wherever the owner's countries are. Against everything else the card does nothing: the anti-bomb appears in no match check (it counts toward no kind and cannot be picked), and F011's reading of the combat handlers found no card read anywhere in an exchange — the protection is only against the bomb. The card's face and its on-screen name are the cast's (T24); the code's own message names it as an anti-bomb.
- **Same rule?** The test and the one-card removal are the same; the compaction is not, and neither is the hand it acts on. The computer rewrites and compacts the **victim owner's** hand; the human removes the victim's anti-bomb from the victim's slot but its reorder call compacts the **bomber's** hand (the current-turn seat, with the trade-in's stale picks), leaving the victim's hand with the emptied slot and able to clear the bomber's retained cards a second time. The protection rule is shared; the consumption side effect is human-only.
- **Sources:** `static:decompile` `Islepro.exe` sha256 `c4d7a248646454adeb48232e3ac7b3d2c0f08b290051a79d6fe6e47bb76b594e`; `BehaviorScript 400` / `mouseDown()` (the human bomb branch's protection test, announcement and removal); `MovieScript 333 ComputerPlaceArmies` / `computerplacearmies()` (the computer bomb branch's same test, announcement and removal); `MovieScript 532 Reordercards` / `Reordercards()` (the shared removal and compaction the human's branch calls; it reads the current-turn seat and the marked picks, so it acts on the bomber, not the victim); `MovieScript 75` / `PutinInfo()` (the refreshed counts).
- **What would change it.** A second reader of kind 2 would change "only against the bomb" (none found in the 213 decompiled scripts; F011's combat handlers read no card at all).
- **T28.** Bomb a seat known to hold the anti-bomb (at least 3 cases): expect the protection message, the armies unchanged, one anti-bomb gone from the panel count. Bomb the same seat again after the card is spent (expect the halving). Record **both hands** around each protected bombing: for the computer's branch the victim's hand is compacted (the anti-bomb slot is gone and the remaining cards slide forward); for the human's branch the victim's hand keeps the emptied slot and the bomber's own hand is the one compacted — check whether the bomber's retained cards are cleared a second time when its trade-in picks are still marked, and whether a retained anti-bomb is among them: stage a hand of three bomb-match cards, an anti-bomb and a +15, the first three picked, so that after the trade-in the anti-bomb and the +15 slide into the first two slots and the second reorder call can clear them (including the bomber's own anti-bomb).

## Observations

### The card-kind table (for later tasks and for malpaco)

The deck is the 34-entry list below; a draw is a uniform pick among its entries (the bomb's entry excluded for an empty hand), with replacement. "Trade-in adds" is what a set of three of the kind adds to the pool (F013's numbers, here with the kinds).

| Kind | Cards in the deck | Draw chance (non-empty hand) | Role | Trade-in adds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 3 | 3/34 (about 8.8%) | the bomb-match card | nothing; arms the bomb |
| 2 | 2 | 2/34 (about 5.9%) | the anti-bomb: consumed to cancel one bomb against its owner | counts toward no match; cannot be picked |
| 3 | 8 | 8/34 (about 23.5%) | the weakest army card | +5 armies |
| 4 | 6 | 6/34 (about 17.6%) | army card | +8 armies |
| 5 | 3 | 3/34 (about 8.8%) | the strongest army card | +15 armies |
| 6 | 6 | 6/34 (about 17.6%) | the wildcard: at most two count toward one kind | joins a set of kinds 1, 3, 4, 5 or 8 |
| 7 | 1 | 1/34 (about 2.9%); never with an empty hand | the bomb: drawn — the drawer's whole hand is emptied; never occupies a hand slot | not a match card |
| 8 | 5 | 5/34 (about 14.7%) | the doubling card | doubles the whole pool |

With an empty hand the chances are the same counts over 33 (kind 3 about 24.2%, the bomb 0).

### The draw rules (for later tasks and for malpaco)

1. Every hand starts the game at five empty slots (the new-game reset; no card is dealt).
2. A seat draws once, and only once, in a turn in which it captured at least one country (the capture flag); a failed human attack cancels the turn's draw. The human draws at the end of its turn, on a screen; the computer after its move inside its turn handler, silently.
3. The draw is uniform over the 34-entry fixed list (33 with an empty hand, excluding the bomb); the deck never depletes, reshuffles or refuses.
4. The drawn card takes the first empty slot of the hand.
5. A full hand (five) never draws directly: the human is routed through a discard screen (one card of the player's choice, by clicking), the computer discards by priority — anti-bomb, then bomb-match card, then +5 card, else a random slot. Net: discard one, draw one.
6. Drawing the bomb empties the drawer's whole hand and gives nothing else; the bomb can never be drawn from an empty hand and never occupies a hand slot afterwards.
7. A trade-in removes three cards and compacts the hand, cards sliding to the front. The human removes exactly the three it picked. The computer walks the hand in slot order and removes the first three cards that hold the chosen kind or a wildcard, with no cap on the wildcards among them (so a set detected with two capped wildcards can consume three wildcards and keep the kind card — Q-BON-07's corner).
8. A dead seat's hand is wiped (F012); the anti-bomb is consumed by the first bomb its owner suffers.

### The two paths side by side

| | Human | Computer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Draw moment | end of its turn, on the card screen | after its move, inside the turn handler |
| Draw | one per capturing turn; uniform over the 34-entry list (33 with an empty hand) | the same |
| Full hand | forced: a notice, then one card discarded by clicking, then the draw | automatic: anti-bomb first, then bomb-match card, then +5, else a random slot; then the draw |
| Bomb drawn | the whole hand is emptied | the same |
| Trade-in | a screen at the top of the turn (after income, before placement): pick three, trade or skip; invalid picks refused, nothing consumed | automatic, same place in the turn: one set by fixed priority (15, 8, double, 5, bomb), never skipped |
| Wildcards | up to two count toward one kind in detection; three wildcards alone are no match; anti-bombs unpickable; exactly the picked three are removed | the same detection and cap; the removal is not capped — it can consume three wildcards for one army card (Q-BON-07) |
| Bomb target | any enemy country, by clicking (own countries refused; no adjacency rule) | nine draws over its front-line enemy countries; the comparison indexes the army table with a sample's army count, so no candidate-stack comparison (Q-BON-08) |
| Bomb effect | the victim's armies replaced by a draw of 1 to half; owner unchanged; pool untouched | the same |
| Anti-bomb | cancels one bomb against its owner, consumed; the victim's slot is emptied but its hand is not compacted, and the bomber's hand is compacted a second time with the stale picks (Q-BON-09) | cancels one bomb against its owner, consumed; the owner's hand is compacted in place |

### Where random numbers are drawn (Randomness)

- **The card draw:** one draw of 1 to 34 (or 1 to 33 with an empty hand) per capturing turn, per seat — the only draw in either draw handler.
- **The full-hand fallback (computer):** one draw of 1 to 5, only when its hand holds no anti-bomb, no bomb-match card and no +5 card.
- **The human's bomb:** one draw of 1 to half the victim's armies (the victim pick is the player's click).
- **The computer's bomb:** nine draws over its front-line candidate list (the victim pick), then the same one draw of 1 to half.
- The generator and its distribution belong to Director's runtime (T18); the chances above assume it is uniform.

### Agreement with the documents

`Islepro.txt` (`knowledge-map.md`, Cards and Trading in cards rows): "captured a country then you will be given a card" (agrees: Q-BON-05; the code adds the one-per-turn limit and the failed-attack cancellation); "A match is 3 cards which are the same" (agrees in kind: Q-BON-07; the code adds the wildcard exception — up to two wildcards count toward a kind, so a match need not be three of the same); sets "must be exchanged at the top of your turn" (agrees: both trade-ins run after income and before placement, F013's order); "You can only hold 5 cards in your hand at once" (agrees: Q-BON-06; the code adds how a full hand is enforced per seat). The transcription quotes no card types, so the kind table above is the only attributed source. F003's captured help text — bombing any country diminishes its troops — agrees with the bomb's target rule and effect (Q-BON-08), and the code adds the halving draw, the own-country refusal and the anti-bomb. malpaco's `RULES.md` guesses ("Remove 2 armies"; "reshuffled from the discard when empty"; "A draw into a full hand is discarded") match none of the three: Pro redraws at half, never reshuffles (the deck is a fixed table), and a full hand discards an old card before drawing a new one.

## Inferences

- **Attacking pays twice.** The capture draw is the only source of cards, and cards are the only source of the doubling match, which multiplies the whole turn's income. An economy that never attacks earns only its floor and islands; one that captures draws from a table whose best entry (the doubling card, 5 of 34 with any kind pair) compounds. From Q-BON-04, Q-BON-05 and Q-BON-07.
- **The bomb is a softener, not a killer.** Redrawing at half never empties a country and never moves ownership; its worth is highest against a large stack (a 40-stack becomes at most 20) and nil against a 1-stack. The computer's victim pick is noisier than it looks: it makes nine draws over its front-line list but its comparison reads the army table at an index taken from a sampled stack rather than comparing the candidate stacks, so which victim it favours is not established here (T28 records it) — either way it is the opposite of malpaco's flat "remove 2". From Q-BON-08.
- **The anti-bomb prices the bomb.** Two anti-bombs in 34 cards, consumed on use, protecting their owner's every country: a bomb traded against a prepared seat is wasted (nothing halves, the protection is gone, the set is spent). The human can hold and time it; the computer sheds its anti-bomb first when its hand is full — the machine under-values its own protection. From Q-BON-09 and Q-BON-06.
- **The deck never runs dry, so the draw chance is stable all game.** Late-game draws have the same weights as early ones; no exhaustion strategy exists, and T28's tabulations pool safely across game phases. From Q-BON-04.
- **The computer never skips a trade and never doubles past its priority.** With both a 15-set and a doubling set it takes the 15 (F013's handicap, now with kinds: kind 5 before kind 8); with only a doubling set it doubles. A malpaco AI that always compares values would diverge from Pro's machine — a deliberate difference to decide, not an accident to copy. From Q-BON-07.

## Alternatives considered

- **The deck might be a real deck (dealt without replacement).** Excluded: neither draw handler removes the drawn entry, no second structure tracks drawn cards, and the list's only writer is the movie-start literal. The "shuffle when empty" that malpaco guesses has no counterpart: the list is never written again.
- **The empty-hand short draw might exclude a card other than the bomb.** The exclusion is the list's last position, and the literal's last entry is the bomb (kind 7); the bomb's own branch (the hand wipe) is the only kind-specific behaviour in either draw handler. Bytecode-confirmed at the literal and the bound.
- **Three wildcards might be a match.** Excluded by the cap: the wildcard count is clamped to two before it joins any kind's count, in all three match checks (turn-start human, trade-in button, computer). Three wildcards alone leave every kind at two.
- **The trade-in might be repeatable.** The only jump to the trade-in screen is the turn-start frame's; both button exits and the bomb's return lead to placement. A score-level loop cannot be excluded (frame order not decoded) — stated at 90%, not excluded.
- **The doubling add-gate might be a second cap on the pool.** The gate is on the written bonus value only (below 200 and above 0): the doubling's bonus is the earned number itself, so an earned number of 200 or more would fail the gate and the trade would consume nothing and add nothing while showing the trading message. Unreachable in ordinary play — the earned number is at most 15 (the country part of a 46-country board, F013) plus 35 (all nine islands) = 50 — so it is a corner of the code, not a pool cap.
- **The computer might bomb any country, as the human can.** Excluded by its branch's candidate list: only enemy countries bordering the computer's own countries enter it. The human's click, by contrast, is restricted only against its own countries.
- **The anti-bomb might protect the bombed country's neighbours or a whole island.** Excluded: both branches test the bombed country's owner's hand, nothing else.
- **The bomb might also destroy the anti-bomb of a seat other than the victim.** Half excluded as read: the anti-bomb removal rewrites only the victim owner's hand, and the human's later reorder call acts on the bomber's own hand (the current-turn seat), not on a third seat's. But that reorder call has no card-kind exemption — it clears every still-picked position of the current-turn hand before compacting — so it can destroy the **bomber's own** anti-bomb: a pick of three bomb-match cards with an anti-bomb and a +15 behind them compacts the retained anti-bomb and +15 into the first two slots, and the reorder call after a protected bombing then clears those retained positions, anti-bomb included (Q-BON-09). What a particular hand and pick pattern leaves at runtime is the T28 both-hands check's (the runtime caveat of that answer stands).
- **The computer's bomb might pick the largest candidate stack.** Excluded as read: the nine-sample comparison indexes the army table with a sampled stack instead of comparing candidate stacks (Q-BON-08); what it does pick at runtime is left to T28.
- **A decompiler misrender of the deck literal or the adds.** Made unlikely by the bytecode listings (the same 34-entry literal; the bonus immediates 5, 8, 15 and 1000), not excluded by a second decompiler.

## What this does not establish

- **That the decompiler is faithful in every handler.** One decompiler (ProjectorRays 0.2.0); bytecode comparisons were made at the deck literal, the trade-in's four bonus values, the wildcard cap and both draw bounds, and no run of this finding corroborates any behaviour.
- **Runtime behaviour and the score.** The frame order — the card screen chain (title, full-hand branch, discard loop, draw), the trade-in screen chain (display, pick, loop), the bombing chain (arm, click, return) — and which sprite carries which script are not decoded: every "routes to" here rests on the jumps each handler makes, read one by one, as in F011 and F012. The human's once-per-turn trade-in and the full-hand detour's reachability carry that caveat.
- **The registered build,** and anything gated by the upgrade check or the registration global (rule 19). No registration test was found inside any card handler read, but what registration unlocks elsewhere is `unknown`.
- **The card faces, names and sounds.** Which picture or sound belongs to which kind is in the cast (T24); the mechanics here are the code's, the look is not read.
- **Measured draw frequencies, trade-in frequencies and bomb frequencies** (T28 counts them). Every chance above is a ratio of list lengths, not an observation.
- **The computer's placement and attack choices** (T27): the candidate list and the nine-draw pick are read here because they are the bombing branch; the rest of its scoring is T27's.
- **Two corners of the code:** the computer bombing with no front-line enemy country (an empty-list read, left unestablished), and the doubling trade at an earned number of 200 or more (unreachable at the board's maximum of 50, Q-BON-07's corner).
- **What the computer's bomb comparison does at runtime.** The comparison's index is a sampled stack used as an army-table index (Q-BON-08); the reading establishes the indexing from the code and the bytecode, not which victim the nine samples leave or how Director handles a void or out-of-range lookup, so the computer's target distribution is left unestablished (T28 records it).
- **What the human's anti-bomb reorder call does at runtime.** The shared reorder call is entered with the current-turn seat and stale trade-in picks (Q-BON-09); the source shows which lists it rewrites, not how a particular hand and pick pattern end up, which the hand check in T28 records.
- **DOS Isle Wars.** Nothing here is claimed for it (rule 19); its card rules stay as the DOS findings left them.
