## Answer

Every source below is `static:decompile` of `Islepro.exe`, sha256 `c4d7a248646454adeb48232e3ac7b3d2c0f08b290051a79d6fe6e47bb76b594e`; scripts are cited as in `questions/pro-static.md` (kind, number, name when it has one, handler). Nothing is quoted: the rules are described in our words (rule 18). "The human" is the code that runs for a seat marked human in the four-seat opponent table (the unregistered build's default marks seat 1 human and seats 2 to 4 computer, and the turn marker is switched by the same table); "the computer" is the code that runs for a seat marked computer. Globals that recur: the seat whose turn it is; the 46-entry ownership table (country number to seat); the 46-entry army table (country number to armies); the four per-seat country lists; the earned number of this turn; the pool of armies left to place; the island-bonus sum of this turn; the list of islands completely held; and the nine island member tables, built once at movie start.

**In one paragraph.** Income is computed once per turn, in the turn-start frame, immediately after the turn advance has moved the seat and rebuilt its country list: the seat's country count is divided by 3 with integer division (rounded down), that part is raised to 2 when below 2, and then the fixed bonus of every island the seat holds completely is added — the floor never touches the bonus part, and the bonus is never rounded because it is a sum of whole numbers. Nothing else joins the sum: the handler reads no production centre and no difficulty setting, and captures made later in the turn do not feed back into it. A card trade-in, when one is made, is the only addition after income: it happens after the income computation and before any army is placed, and it adds 5, 8 or 15 armies, or doubles the whole pool (bonus included), or — for the bombing match — adds nothing and detours through a bombing click. The human's trade-in screen appears only at that point, only when its hand holds a valid three-of-a-kind, and can be skipped by a button; the computer trades in automatically, one set, by a fixed priority. Placement then spends the pool: the human, on frames of its own, clicks any of its countries in any order and moves any part of the remaining pool into each (a counter with plus, minus and all buttons, capped at the pool), and the placement frame loops until the pool is exactly zero — there is no done button and no way to keep an army for later; the computer spends the whole pool inside its one placement handler, on countries its own scoring picks (T27), one army at a time or in fractional splits with the whole remainder last. The pool itself is zeroed and recomputed from scratch at every turn start, so nothing carries over. The nine islands partition the 46 countries into consecutive groups of 3, 5, 5, 10, 3, 7, 3, 7 and 3, worth 2, 4, 4, 8, 2, 5, 2, 5 and 3; a bonus needs every country of the island, partial control earns nothing, and several complete islands add up. No group is a single country, and the code holds exactly one board.

### Income

#### Q-REI-01 How many reinforcement armies does a player receive at the start of a turn as a function of the number of provinces held (divisor, rounding, any cap)?

- **As read (about 95%, one mechanism for both sides).** The country-based part is the seat's country count divided by 3, rounded down: the bytecode shows an integer division, and Director's integer division of two whole numbers truncates, so 1 to 5 countries give 0 or 1 (raised to the floor, Q-REI-02), 6 give 2, 12 give 4, 45 give 15. There is no cap on this part and none on the pool: the largest country-based income on the board is 46 divided by 3, that is 15, only because the board has 46 countries. The count used is the length of the seat's own country list as rebuilt by the turn advance moments earlier, so it includes everything captured up to the end of the previous seat's turn; captures made during the seat's own turn never raise that turn's income, because income is computed once, at turn start, and nothing recomputes it later. The only caller of the income handler is the turn-start frame, right after the advance call, once per turn; the handler exits without computing anything when the game-over flag is already set, and when its own fresh count of the ownership table finds the seat at all 46 countries it sets the game-over flag and exits before any income (the flag's complete setter list is F012's Q-VIC-07). No first-turn exception exists: the frame runs the same for every seat's every turn. `Islepro.txt` agrees ("given armies for the number of countries you occupy. That number is divided by 3", `knowledge-map.md`, Reinforcement and resources).
- **Same rule?** Yes: one shared handler for the human and the computer, with no difficulty term and no seat-dependent quantity. What differs is only the message wording — both sides are told the island count and the number to place, but the human seat's message is an instruction to place and the computer seat's announces that the game is placing — and the counting animation, which runs only at the slower speed setting and is display alone.

#### Q-REI-02 Is there a minimum income per turn however little the player holds, what is it, and does it apply at one, two and three provinces?

- **As read (about 95%, one mechanism for both sides).** Yes: a floor of 2, on the country-based part alone. The handler tests the divided number against 2 with a strict less-than and raises it to 2 — so at 1, 2 and 3 countries (divisions giving 0, 0 and 1) the country-based income is 2 in all three cases, and it stays 2 at 4 and 5 countries, reaching 3 only at 9. #### Q-REI-04 How do the parts of income combine (province-based income, group bonuses, centres, cards), and are the group and centre parts subject to the minimum or the rounding?

- **As read (about 90% for the combination and order, 85% for the centre absence, both sides where not said).** Four candidate parts, three of which exist:
  - **Country part:** the count divided by 3 rounded down, floored at 2 (Q-REI-01, Q-REI-02). Rounding and floor apply to this part only.
  - **Island part:** the sum of the fixed bonuses of the islands completely held (Q-BON-01, Q-BON-02), added after the floor. It is a sum of whole numbers, so no rounding touches it, and the floor neither applies to it nor is applied to the total.
  - **Centres:** no centre part exists. The income handler's global list holds no production-centre table and its body reads none; the centre handlers that do exist belong to the event code (T23). This is an absence claim over a fully read handler, and it matches the question map's note for Q-CEN-02.
  - **Cards:** a trade-in adds to the pool after income and before placement, once per turn. The human's turn-start frame checks its hand for a valid three-of-a-kind (up to two wildcards count toward any kind); when one exists the flow detours to a trade-in screen where the player picks three cards and either trades or skips with a second button. A traded set adds by kind: 5 armies for one kind, 8 for another, 15 for a third, and for the fourth kind the current earned number itself — a doubling of the whole pool, island bonus included, because the earned number at that moment is the full income. The fifth kind is the bombing match: it adds no army (the addition is gated below the bomb's sentinel value of 1000), hands the hand to the card reorder handler (exactly what it removes is T22's reading) and detours to the bomb label; the script that arms the bombing-click state prompts for an enemy country (the bomb's effect is T22's), and a short frame script returns the flow to placement with the pool unchanged. ### Placement

#### Q-REI-03 Can reinforcements be placed on any owned province in any split, or only on one province or one group; may unplaced armies carry over to a later turn?

- **As read (human, about 85%).** Any owned country, in any split, in any order, but the whole pool and nothing else. The placement phase starts on a frame that resets the selected country and the counter to zero and prompts the player to click a country; a board click in the placement state selects any country the ownership table gives to the seat — a click on any other country is refused with a message, in the seat's colour, telling the player to select a country of that colour, plus the error sound, and nothing else happens. Four buttons then act on the selected country: a counter with a plus button (capped at the pool's remainder), a minus button (floored at 0) and an all button (sets the counter to the whole remainder), and a place button that moves the counter's armies from the pool into the selected country, resets the counter to zero and leaves the pool smaller by the placed amount. The player may repeat this on as many countries as the pool allows, in any order, splitting freely; the place button works a country at a time, but nothing limits how many countries receive armies. The placement frame loops on itself, and the only non-looping jump in its script is gated on the pool reaching exactly zero (the handler issues its loop-back jump even after the exit jump; that the exit takes effect when both run is Director's runtime navigation — one recorded point is not static reading: E002's tour, F003, shows a human turn completing placement and reaching the attack screen, so the loop does end in the running game). The phase cannot be ended early: there is no done, pass or cancel button among the placement scripts, and the turn screen (with the attack, move and pass buttons, F011 and F012) is reached only after the pool is empty. Carry-over is therefore impossible twice over: the phase does not end while armies remain unplaced, and the pool is recomputed from zero at the next turn start anyway (the income handler resets it to zero before setting it to the earned number), so an army not placed this turn could not survive to the next. One written-in corner: the place button caps the receiving country's stored count at 999 and silently discards anything above (unreachable in ordinary play; no pool near that size exists). The done button the question map lists among the placement sources is not a placement control: its handler reads the attack and defence country globals and splits armies between them — it is the confirm of the capture move-in (F011, Q-OWN-01).
- **As read (computer, about 85% that the pool is always spent; the choices are T27's).** The computer places inside one handler, in the turn-start frame, with no screen of its own: after any trade-in add, its scoring builds candidate countries — front-line countries (its own countries with at least one enemy neighbour, from the shared candidate-list handler), with island-completion priorities that read the list of islands it owns, and sometimes the production-centre countries — and then spends the pool either one army at a time over the candidates or, when armies remain (or by the drawn branch), in splits of a third and a half of the remainder with the whole remainder assigned last to one country, so the pool always ends at zero. Its placement respects the same 999 cap per country. Which countries it prefers, and the draws inside the choice, are T27's reading; the income-side fact is that the whole pool is placed in the same frame, before its attacks.
- **Same rule?** Yes in what must be placed (the whole pool, this turn, no carry-over, 999 cap per country); no in how: the human chooses countries and splits freely across frames and cannot proceed until the pool is empty, the computer's choices come from its scoring inside one handler.

### Islands

#### Q-BON-01 Does owning every province of a landmass or group give a bonus, and is anything given for partial ownership?

- **As read (about 95%, one mechanism for both sides).** A bonus requires every country of the island; partial control earns nothing at all. The income handler walks the nine island member tables in order — the tables partition the 46 countries into consecutive ranges, so the walk is a single accumulating counter over the countries 1 to 46 — and for each island it holds a sentinel equal to that island's bonus value; if any country of the island is owned by another seat the sentinel is zeroed, and only an intact sentinel adds its value to the bonus sum, counts the island toward the message's island count and appends the island number to the list of islands held. Several complete islands stack: their bonuses sum, with no diminishing and no set-of-islands extra. The message shown to the player reports how many islands the seat holds — the no-island variant is the one F003 captured on screen — not the values. The list of islands held is also read by the computer's placement and attack handlers as a scoring input (T27's); the bonus itself flows only into the pool. Holding all nine islands would add all 35 bonus armies; holding all 46 countries ends the game inside the income handler itself, before that total is computed (its fresh-count test runs first, and exits; F012, Q-VIC-07). `Islepro.txt` agrees ("Each island gives you extra armies, if you totally control all its countries", `knowledge-map.md`, Bonuses).

## Method

- **Artifact and tools.** The decompiled scripts of F010's run `pro-r1` (ProjectorRays 0.2.0 on the movie of `Islepro.exe`, sha256 above), read in place on the owner's machine in the folder `$IWA_STATIC` names; nothing was copied into the repository (rule 18). The scripts' hashes (`ls_sha256`) are those of `experiments/E008-static-triage/measurements/iwp-extract-pro-r1.json`; E011 lists the 24 scripts read with them, and `experiments/E011-pro-reinforcement-reading/check_cited_scripts.py` checks that this finding cites no script, name or handler the record does not have, and (with `--disk`) that each listed script on disk, hashed in place, has the record's hash.
- **What was read.** In full: the income handler; the start handler (board, island and key tables, delay helpers); the turn advance; the turn-start frame with the human and computer branches; the two placement frames; the board click handler (its placement and bomb states; the rest is F011's and T22's); the four placement buttons; the capture move-in confirm and the move confirm (to separate them from placement); the move-counter frame; both card-match checks; the human trade-in button and its skip button; the bomb-arming and bomb-return frames; the computer's front-line candidate handler. In part: the computer's placement handler (903 lines) — its trade-in head, its conquest tests and its pool-spending tail in full, its middle country-choice scoring skimmed (T27); the capture frames' scratch writes to the pool global at the write line and its context (both scripts were read in full for F011/E009). Bytecode comparisons: the income handler's integer division, its strict floor test, the floor-before-bonus order, and the island-9 sentinel of 3 were each confirmed in the bytecode listing beside the decompiled text; so was the placement loop frame's double-jump structure (the conditional exit jump followed by the unconditional loop-back jump, Q-REI-03).
- **What was searched, in the terminal only and with nothing kept.** Every caller of the income handler (one: the turn-start frame); every writer of the pool global across the decompiled scripts (nine scripts, all classified under Observations); every reader of the earned number and of the islands-held list; every setter of the island tables (one: the start handler); the setters of the opponent table (the seat toggles and the defaults handler); and every reference to the placement, trade-in and bomb frame labels (five handlers, all cited above).

- **Static first (rule 24).** This finding is the static answer; no run was made for it. ## Observations

### The island table (for later tasks and for malpaco)

Countries are the board's numbers 1 to 46; each island is one consecutive range. Outside crossings are directed border entries pointing at another island, counted from the border tables (the tables are symmetric: every crossing is listed on both sides).

| Island | Countries | Size | Bonus | Outside crossings (to islands) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 to 3 | 3 | 2 | 2 (2, 3) |
| 2 | 4 to 8 | 5 | 4 | 3 (1, 3, 8) |
| 3 | 9 to 13 | 5 | 4 | 4 (1, 2, 4) |
| 4 | 14 to 23 | 10 | 8 | 6 (3, 5, 8, 9) |
| 5 | 24 to 26 | 3 | 2 | 3 (4, 6) |
| 6 | 27 to 33 | 7 | 5 | 4 (5, 7, 8) |
| 7 | 34 to 36 | 3 | 2 | 2 (6, 8) |
| 8 | 37 to 43 | 7 | 5 | 5 (2, 4, 6, 7, 9) |
| 9 | 44 to 46 | 3 | 3 | 3 (4, 8) |

Totals: 46 countries, 35 bonus armies. Bonus against size: 3 pays 2 (islands 1, 5, 7), 5 pays 4, 7 pays 5, 10 pays 8 — about three quarters of the size, rounded — except island 9, where 3 countries pay 3.

### The income formula (for later tasks and for malpaco)

For the seat whose turn starts, in this order, once per turn:

1. count = the seat's country count (its rebuilt country list's length);
2. base = count divided by 3, integer division (rounded down);
3. if base < 2 then base = 2 (the floor; strict; the country part only);
4. bonus = the sum of the fixed values of the islands the seat holds completely (floor not applied, nothing rounded);
5. pool = base + bonus — the number the message shows and placement must spend;
6. trade-in (only when a set is traded this turn): pool = pool + 5, + 8 or + 15 by the kind, or pool = 2 × pool for the doubling kind, or no change for the bombing kind;
7. placement spends the pool to exactly zero (human: on its frames, forced; computer: inside its one handler).

No production-centre term, no difficulty term, no event term and no cap enters the sum; captures during the turn do not recompute it.

### The turn's arithmetic, in order (both sides)

| Step | Human | Computer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Advance | the turn-start frame calls the advance (seat moved, dead seats skipped, country lists rebuilt, the 46 test) | the same call, the same frame |
| Income | the income handler: formula above; its message instructs the player to place the earned number | the same handler; its message announces the same number as being placed; the counting animation runs only at the slow speed setting |
| Cards | a match check flags the hand; with a match, the flow detours to the trade-in screen: pick three, trade (adds now) or skip (adds nothing); the bomb match detours through the bombing click and adds nothing | its match check picks at most one set by fixed priority (15, then 8, then doubling, then 5, then bomb) and removes the cards; its placement handler adds the bonus or doubles the pool, announcing the trade-in and its effect in a status message |
| Placement | its own frames: click own countries, counter buttons, place button; the frame loops until the pool is zero; no early exit | inside the turn-start frame's handler: candidates scored, pool spent one by one or in splits, always to zero |
| Then | the turn screen: attack, move, pass (F011, F012) | its attack rounds, one move, a card draw on capture (F011, F012) — all in the same frame |

### The pool global's two lives (a measurement trap for T28)

The pool global is reused as scratch by the battle and move flows after placement: the capture frame (`BehaviorScript 60` / `exitFrame()`) sets it to the attacker's remaining armies minus one for the move-in split, the computer's dice handler (`MovieScript 337 computerrolldice` / `ComputerRollDice()`) sets it to the attacker's armies at a capture, the move-counter frame (`BehaviorScript 100` / `exitFrame()`) sets it to the source's armies minus one, and the two confirm buttons (`CastScript 96 DONE2` / `mouseDown()`, `CastScript 332 DONE3` / `mouseDown()`) zero it. None of this can touch reinforcement (placement precedes attack and move in the frame chain, and the income handler zeroes and recomputes the pool at every turn start), but a runtime probe that reads the global mid-turn will see battle scratch, not income. The writers found by the search, besides these: the income handler, the human trade-in, the place button and the computer placement handler (all cited above).

### Where random numbers are drawn (Randomness)

None in income: the income handler draws nothing — the formula, the floor and the island tests are deterministic. Draws adjacent to this finding belong elsewhere: the computer's placement branches and candidate choices (T27), the bomb victim pick inside the computer's bombing branch (a pick among nine random draws over its front-line list, T22/T27), the card draws (T22) and the battles (F011).

### Agreement with the documents

`Islepro.txt` (knowledge-map.md, Reinforcement and resources and Bonuses rows): armies "for the number of countries you occupy. That number is divided by 3" (agrees: Q-REI-01); "(Minimum is 2)" for the turn's armies (agrees: Q-REI-02; the fragment does not say the floor applies before the island bonus, which the code adds); "Each island gives you extra armies, if you totally control all its countries" (agrees: Q-BON-01); "number of armies to place onto the board" (agrees: the pool is placed, not auto-added). The directions' island-value figure names no island, so it neither confirms nor contradicts the table above (Q-BON-02). The observed board message "Blue has no islands." (F003, run E002) matches the island-count message the income handler builds. malpaco's `RULES.md` guesses a per-province divisor and a post-divisor floor (its `reinforcement.perProvinceDivisor` and `reinforcement.minimum` rows): the code's divisor is 3 and the floor is 2, applied post-divisor and pre-bonus.

## Inferences

- **Whether holding islands pays.** Income per country is about one third of an army, but a complete island roughly doubles its countries' yield: 10 countries with no island earn 3 + 0, while 10 countries that are exactly island 4 earn 3 + 8 = 11 — more than three times the islandless figure for the same count. Against F011's exchange odds (an even-stack attack captures about 11 to 20% of the time, F011's Q-CMB-12 decomposition), island bonuses are the main positive term in whether attacking pays: they reward completing a group, which requires taking countries from their holder. From Q-BON-01, Q-BON-02 and Q-REI-01.
- **The floor keeps a beaten seat alive.** A seat cut to 1 to 5 countries still earns 2 a turn, so elimination by starvation does not exist; only capture removes a seat. From Q-REI-02.
- **Trade-in armies are same-turn armies.** Because the trade-in add lands in the pool before placement, card bonuses are placed and can attack in the same turn — the doubling match on a large income (say 20) is worth more than any fixed add. The computer's fixed priority (15 before doubling) forgoes that above a pool of 15: a deliberate handicap or an oversight, T27's to weigh. From Q-REI-04.
- **Island 9's extra army.** The 3-country island 9 pays 3 where the size pattern says 2. Island 9 is a corner of the board (its countries touch only islands 4 and 8); the extra army may price that isolation, or be a slip. Either way it is fixed in code, and malpaco can set its own values knowing Pro's are hand-set, size-correlated constants. From Q-BON-02.

## Alternatives considered

- **The division might be floating-point, giving fractional income.** Excluded: the bytecode shows the integer division opcode, and the earned number goes straight into a text message and an army count with no rounding step anywhere else.
- **The floor of 2 might apply to the total (country part plus bonus).** Excluded by the bytecode's order: the strict less-than test and the raise to 2 come before the bonus add. A seat whose 3 countries are exactly one complete 2-bonus island earns 4; flooring after the bonus would give 3.
- **Island 9's bonus might be a decompiler artifact (2 misread as 3).** Checked: the sentinel appears as 3 in the decompiled text and as an immediate 3 in the bytecode listing of that branch.
- **The done button (CastScript 96) might end placement with armies left.** Excluded: its handler reads the attack and defence country globals and splits between them — the capture move-in of F011's Q-OWN-01; the placement loop frame's only exit is the pool reaching zero.
- **Unplaced armies might carry over.** Excluded twice: the human's placement frame loops until the pool is zero, and the income handler zeroes and recomputes the pool at every turn start, so no stale value could survive even if a frame were skipped.
- **The placement loop might never end, if Director honoured the unconditional loop-back jump over the conditional exit jump issued before it.** Excluded by the one runtime point (F003's recorded human turn: placement completed and the attack screen followed), and no human turn could ever finish under the other resolution.
- **Bonuses might scale with size at runtime.** Excluded: nine fixed sentinel values in the loop; no multiplication or table lookup by size anywhere in the handler.
- **A second board, or a single-country bonus group, might exist.** Not excluded with certainty (the 61 unwritten scripts, the registered build): the search found one setter of the island tables and no generator in the 213 decompiled scripts. Stated as an absence claim at about 85%.
- **The human's trade-in might be repeatable in one turn.** The only jump to the trade-in screen label found is the turn-start frame's, and both trade-in exits and the bomb return lead to placement, so as read it is once per turn; a path through the score that re-enters the screen cannot be excluded (frame order not decoded), which is why the timing figure is 90% and not higher.

## What this does not establish

- **That the decompiler is faithful in every handler.** One decompiler (ProjectorRays 0.2.0); bytecode comparisons were made for the income handler's division, floor test, floor-before-bonus order and the island-9 sentinel, and for the placement loop frame's jump structure, and no run of this finding corroborates any behaviour (the single cited runtime point is F003's recorded turn, flagged in Q-REI-03).
- **Runtime behaviour and the score.** The frame order and which sprite carries which button are not decoded: the chains here (turn-start to trade-in screen to placement to turn screen; the bomb detour) rest on the jumps each handler makes, read one by one, as in F012. The messages are the text fields the scripts set, not observed screens.
- **The registered build,** and anything gated by the upgrade check or the registration global (rule 19). No registration test was found inside the income, placement or trade-in handlers read, but what registration unlocks elsewhere (multiple human seats, each earning by the same formula) is `unknown`.
- **The cards themselves:** kinds, deck composition, draws, hand limit and the bomb's effect (T22); only the numbers a trade-in adds to the pool are claimed here.
- **Production centres:** their placement, events and effects (T23); only the absence of a centre term in the income handler.
- **The computer's choices:** which countries it places on, with what weights and draws (T27); only that its whole pool is spent, before its attacks.
- **The deal and starting positions** (T18): Q-MAP-13's second half is not answered here.
- **DOS Isle Wars.** Nothing here is claimed for it (rule 19).
- **Any observed rate or frequency:** every number here is a constant or a count in the code, not a measurement (T28).
