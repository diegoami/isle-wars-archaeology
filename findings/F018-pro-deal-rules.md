## Answer

Every source below is `static:decompile` of `Islepro.exe`, sha256 `c4d7a248646454adeb48232e3ac7b3d2c0f08b290051a79d6fe6e47bb76b594e`; scripts are cited as in `questions/pro-static.md` (kind, number, name when it has one, handler). Nothing is quoted: the rules are described in our words (rule 18), and the deal's numbers are cited only as the checked output of `experiments/E016-pro-deal-reading/check_deal_constants.py` or as plain values (rule 20). "Seat" is 1 to 4; the opponent table's entry for a seat is 1 for a human and 0 for a computer. The deal runs over the fixed 46-country board of F017 (C-BRD-01, C-BRD-02); the per-country numbering below is that board's. A "draw of 1 to n" is how Director's random function is read; the generator itself is Director's runtime (Q-RND-02, T34) and no distribution is claimed beyond uniformity of the function's documented range.

**In one paragraph.** A fresh game runs a fixed chain: the new-game reset swaps every country's piece back to its empty-country image and empties the card hands, a display frame makes the board visible and announces the setup phase, and one frame script then does the whole starting state in three calls — the deal, the centre placement, the first-seat selection. The deal handler first zeroes the game-level state (the game-over, quit, fast-forward, scoring and turn-local globals), then fills a list to 46 entries by drawing seats and appending only computer seats' draws, under a soft per-seat cap of 15 that is counted on first draws and applied by up to two redraw passes; each human seat then overwrites exactly its level's number of entries (5, 9, 14 or 16; 11 in the registered all-human branch this build cannot reach); the list is shuffled by repeated draw-and-remove; and a per-country pass builds the ownership table, the per-seat country lists, the display, and one army draw of 1 to the starting-armies option per country — an option forced to 3 in the unregistered build. The centres are three uniform distinct countries drawn from a full-board shuffle of the same shape. The first-seat selection is an animated draw of 9 to 18 iterations whose stored result is decremented by one seat at the end, so the seat that moves first is not the seat the marker last showed. Nothing reads or writes any other file at game start: there is no scenario or saved position to load. What varies between games is exactly the deal, the armies, the centres and the first seat — the board never does.

### The deal

#### Q-STA-01 How are the territories dealt to the seats?

- **As read (about 90%).** One handler does all of it, called once per game from the start-of-game frame script (the only caller, found by search). In order:
  - **The reset.** Before any draw the handler zeroes the game-level state: the quit-early flag, the fast-forward flag, the game counter, the stale board-click distance, the game-over flag, the action and clicked-country globals, and the do-not-score counter — which it sets to the number of human seats (F012's Q-SUR-05 reads that counter at the game's end; here is where it is born). It rebuilds the four-entry copy of the opponent table that play reads, empties the deal list, and moves the board marker off-screen.
  - **The fill loop.** While the deal list holds fewer than 46 entries: draw a seat (1 to 4); increment that seat's draw counter; apply the cap (two passes, Q-MAP-10 below); and append the final value to the deal list only when it names a computer seat. The entry count advances only on an append, so the loop repeats — with draws on human seats wasted — until the list holds 46. In the unregistered build the opponent table is forced to one human at seat 1 (Q-AI-03), so only seats 2, 3 and 4 can be appended: the fill is a capped, uneven-in-time split of the 46 entries among the three computer seats.
  - **The all-human branch.** If all four seats are human, the fill loop is skipped (the entry count is pre-set to 46) and the list is instead filled with 46 uniform draws over all four seats, with no cap, and the minimum below becomes 11 regardless of the level. The unregistered build cannot reach this branch: the defaults forcing and the locked seat toggles (Q-AI-03) always leave exactly one human. It is stated here because the handler carries it, not analysed: its balance is a registered-build question (rule 19, out of scope).
  - **The minimum overwrite.** A single value is taken from the level: 5 at level 1, 9 at level 2, 14 at level 3, 16 at level 4 — no branch exists for 5 to 10 (Q-STA-04 below). Then each human seat, in seat order, overwrites the next entries of the deal list with its own number, that many per seat, one shared position counter walking the list. In this build's one-human game, exactly the first *a* entries of the list become the human seat's number.
  - **The shuffle.** The whole list is shuffled by repeated draw-and-remove: while entries remain, one is drawn uniformly from those remaining and moved to a new list. The human's overwritten entries therefore end at uniformly random positions of the board, as do the computers'.
  - **A turn draw that decides nothing.** The handler zeroes the turn global and immediately draws it fresh (1 to 4) — but nothing ever reads that value: the only caller runs the centre placement and then the first-seat selection next, and the selection overwrites the turn global before the game's first turn (Q-STA-03). The draw does advance the game's random generator, so the armies' particular values depend on it, but it decides nothing itself.
  - **The per-country pass.** The handler then walks the board's 46 countries in order. For each it reads the shuffled list's entry (the owner), appends the country to that seat's country list, updates the seat's on-screen country count, swaps the country's piece image for the owner's colour (the seat-to-colour mapping is a four-letter table built at movie start, `MovieScript 1 Startmovie` / `startMovie()`), waits a short fixed delay, and draws the country's starting armies (Q-STA-02), storing it in the army table and writing it to the country's on-board text field. When the pass ends, the ownership table, the army table and the four per-seat country lists are complete and the board is displayed: the deal is done, and the deal list itself is a local of this handler — no other script names it (found by search).
- **The exactness of the human's share (a sharpening of F016's C-AI-08).** In the unregistered build the human seat ends the deal with *exactly* its level's number of countries — not "at least" that number. No entry of the deal list can hold the human's number except through the overwrite: the fill appends computer seats only. F016's wording ("the seat ends with at least that many") is correct only for the all-human branch, where the pre-filled draws can land on any seat; that branch is unreachable here. The human's share in this build is a constant of the level, and only its *positions* are drawn.
- **Same rule?** No. Every seat's countries come from the same deal and the same shape of randomness, but the human's count is fixed by the level while the computers' counts are drawn under the cap; and only the computers' draws are capped at all.

### The cap on territories per player

#### Q-MAP-10 (deal half) What caps the number of territories a player can be dealt, and what happens at the cap?

- **As read (the mechanism about 90%; the resulting distribution is not claimed).** The cap is 15 per seat, and it is a cap on *draws*, not on holdings. The fill loop keeps a per-seat counter of that seat's first draws — incremented on every first draw, before the cap is applied, and whether or not the entry is ever appended. Two identical passes then run, each redrawing the seat once more when the drawn seat's counter exceeds 15; the redrawn value is neither counted nor capped again beyond the second pass. Three consequences follow:
  - The counters overcount: a seat's counter grows for draws that land on the human seat (never appended in this build) and for draws that are redrawn away. Once a seat's counter passes 15, no first draw onto it appends — the draw is redone.
  - An over-cap seat can still receive entries, but only by accident: a redraw can land on it uncounted, and if the second pass's redraw lands on the same over-cap seat again, that value is appended with no third check. So the cap is soft — a strong bias, not a wall.
  - With three computer seats sharing 46 − *a* entries (41, 37, 32 or 30 as the level takes 5, 9, 14 or 16 — arithmetic checked in the record), the cap holds each computer's counter at or below 16, so the counters converge and the seats' actual shares end near-even, ragged by the uncounted redraws. How near-even in practice is a runtime property: T28's to measure, not stated here.
  - No territory starts neutral: every one of the 46 entries names a seat after the fill (F017's board half of this question, confirmed at the deal level — the fill's loop bound and the per-country pass's bound are the same 46).
- **Same rule?** No. The human's share bypasses the cap entirely (it is written by the overwrite, after the fill); only the computers' draws are capped.
- **Sources:** as Q-STA-01, with `MovieScript 5 Putinboard` / `Putinboard()` (the two cap passes and the counters) and `experiments/E016-pro-deal-reading/measurements/deal-constants-r1b.json` (the 8 comparisons at 15 — two passes over four seats — each followed within a few bytecode positions by its own redraw draw, and the share arithmetic).
- **What would change it.** A reading of the cap as a holding cap would change everything above — excluded by the counter's increment-before-redraw order, which the bytecode shows. A misrender of one cap pass would leave 4 comparisons, not 8; the listing shows 8.
- **T28.** Over at least 20 starts (the question map's plan), record the three computer seats' country counts: expect near-even splits of 46 − *a*, each count within the cap's reach (a count above 15 should be rare and is possible only through the double-redraw path — a good signature that the reading is right). Expect the human's count to sit exactly at the level's number every time.

### The starting armies

In the unregistered build the starting-armies option is forced to 3 before any game runs (Q-AI-03), so **every country starts with 1 to 3 armies**, human- and computer-held alike. The difficulty value enters the armies nowhere: the deal handler's only use of it is the minimum branch (Q-STA-04).
- **Same rule?** Yes — one rule for every country and every seat: 1 to the option's value, drawn independently. No seat term, no country term, no difficulty term.

### The setup after the deal

#### Q-STA-03 Does any setup step happen after the deal, and in what order?

- **As read (the call order about 95%; the frame order around it about 70% — combined about 85%).** Yes, two steps, and both are inside the start-of-game frame script, which calls three things in a fixed order: the deal, the centre placement, the first-seat selection (`BehaviorScript 3` / `exitFrame()`, the only caller of all three — found by search). A frame earlier in the chain makes the board visible and announces the setup phase (`BehaviorScript 2` / `exitFrame()`); the start-of-game frame clears the status lines before the deal and announces the centres phase between the deal and the placement. In order:
  - **The centre placement** (`MovieScript 51 putinproductioncenters` / `Putinproductioncenters()`). It builds a list of all 46 countries and shuffles it by the same repeated draw-and-remove as the deal; the entries at positions 12, 24 and 37 of the shuffled list become the three production centres — three uniform, distinct countries anywhere on the board, independent of ownership; each is then announced and coloured in turn. F015's Q-CEN-01 reading, re-derived here in full: the placement runs over the whole board after the deal, so a centre can land on any seat's country.
  - **The first-seat selection** (`MovieScript 399 WHOGOESFIRST` / `WHOGOESFIRST()`). It announces the selection, then draws an iteration count of 9 to 18 (a draw of 1 to 10 plus 8) and iterates: draw a seat (1 to 4), redrawing up to twice while the drawn seat repeats the previous iteration's seat; move the first-player marker to the drawn seat's row; sound the click; store the drawn seat as the previous; and pause — except after the last iteration. When the loop ends, the stored first seat is **decremented by one, wrapping 1 back to 4**. This is the quirk of the starting chain: the game's stored first player is one seat below the seat the marker last displayed. Whether anything later re-syncs the marker to the stored seat is the turn frames' business (F012, T24) and is not settled here; the stored value is what the turn advance uses.
  - **The first turn.** After the selection the frame ends and the game's first turn begins (F012's turn-start frame). Nothing else runs between the deal and the first turn: the deal list leaves no global, the centres and the first seat are the only calls, and no other script calls any of the three (the centre placement has one other caller — the event frame, F015's — which is not in the starting chain).
  - What the code cannot say: which frames these scripts sit between, and where the first turn's frame follows — that is the stage score's, which is not decoded. The call order *inside* the start-of-game frame script is code; everything beyond it is the 15% of this answer that is not code.
- **Same rule?** Not applicable (one starting chain for every game; no option reached in this build changes it — the event frequency is forced, and its frame effects are F015's and T23's).

### No file starts a game

#### Q-STA-05 Can any file load or alter the starting state?

- **As read (about 85%, an absence claim).** No. The game reads exactly two files at start, both created by the movie-start handler when missing (F010's reading, confirmed here): the defaults file and the ciphered scores file. The defaults handler reads the defaults file's eight comma-separated items — the four seats, speed, event frequency, starting armies, difficulty — validates them (no human seat forces the one-human layout; a level of 0 or over 10 becomes 2; a frequency of 0 becomes 2; starting armies of 0 become 3; a speed of 0 becomes 2), and applies the unregistered forcing (one human seat, frequency 2, armies 3); its save writes the same eight items (`MovieScript 458 getdefaults` / `Getdefaults()`, `SaveDefaults()`). The scores reader reads the ciphered six-slot win/loss line (`MovieScript 471 READSCORES` / `READSCORES()`; the cipher pair lives with the registration checker, `MovieScript 480 checkupg` / `CHECKUPG()`, `CIPHER()`, `UNCIPHER()`). No handler opens any other file: there is no scenario file, no saved board, no position file — the starting state is always freshly dealt, and nothing can start a game from anything else. ## Observations

### The deal, step by step

| Step | Decides | Draws |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reset (before the deal) | the game-over, quit, fast-forward, scoring, action and clicked-country globals zeroed; the do-not-score counter set to the human-seat count; the opponent-table copy rebuilt; the marker parked | none |
| Fill loop | the 46 entries, computers only, under the cap | a seat (1 to 4) per entry; up to two redraws when the drawn seat's counter exceeds 15 |
| All-human branch | registered-only: 46 uniform draws over all four seats, minimum 11 | 46 draws of 1 to 4 |
| Minimum | the human's exact share: 5/9/14/16 at levels 1/2/3/4 | none |
| Overwrite | each human seat's entries, in seat order | none |
| Shuffle | the entries' final board positions | a draw of the remaining list's size, 45 times |
| Turn draw | nothing (always overwritten by the first-seat selection) | one draw of 1 to 4 |
| Per-country pass | ownership, the per-seat lists, the display, the armies | one draw of 1 to the armies option, per country |
| Centres (after) | the three production centres | a full-board shuffle; the entries at its 12th, 24th and 37th positions |
| First seat (after) | the game's first player | 9 to 18 iterations of a seat draw with anti-repeat redraws, then a one-seat decrement with wrap |

### The cap's counter subtleties

The cap is best read as three facts. First, the counters count *first draws*, not appends: draws onto the human seat and draws later redrawn away still count, so a seat's counter runs ahead of its actual holdings. Second, a redraw result is appended uncounted, so the counters drift upward relative to the true shares as the fill proceeds. Third, an over-cap seat can still grow — only when two consecutive redraws both land on it, since no third check exists — which is rare but not impossible. The bytecode shows exactly eight comparisons at 15, in four seat-pairs repeated twice, each followed within a few positions by its redraw draw (`deal-constants-r1b.json`). Nothing in the handler caps the human's overwrite: the minimum is written after the fill and is not subject to the cap.

### The exactness of the human's share

C-AI-08 (F016) states the level "guarantees each human seat a minimum of starting countries". In this build the guarantee is exact: the fill never appends the human's number, so the overwrite's *a* entries are the only entries the human can hold. The "at least" wording fits the registered all-human branch only (46 pre-filled draws can land anywhere, and there the minimum is 11 regardless of level). Malpaco should model the unregistered deal as an *exact* share. This is a proposed sharpening of C-AI-08, for promotion after review.

### The two dead and one live oddity of the starting chain

- The deal's turn draw is dead as a value — the first-seat selection overwrites the turn global before anything reads it — but it still consumes one random value, so the armies' particular draws depend on it having happened. A faithful re-implementation keeps the draw (or accepts different army values); a clean-room design would drop it.
- The first-seat selection's final decrement means the stored first player is never the seat the marker last showed. Either the original intends the marker as "the seat that would have gone", or it is an off-by-one; the code cannot say which. T28's first-turn check (Q-STA-03) settles the behaviour; the design choice is malpaco's.
- The new-game reset writes one misspelled global name where its siblings are spelled correctly — the game-over flag's clear in that handler lands on a name nothing declares, so the flag is in fact cleared only by the deal that immediately follows (whose correct clear is part of Q-STA-01's reset list). This is consistent with F012's complete game-over-setter list, which does not include the reset handler. Harmless in the fresh-game flow, since no script reads the flag between the reset and the deal.

### What the unregistered build fixes before any deal

The defaults handler's unregistered forcing runs at every start, before the first game and after every options change: one human seat (seat 1), event frequency 2, starting armies 3. The seat toggles and the armies option refuse clicks with the registered-only notice; the level is not forced. So the deal this finding describes always runs with exactly one human, 1-to-3 armies, and the level's exact share for that human — the options around it are fixed furniture in this build.

## Inferences

- **The deal is a two-stage draw, and malpaco can take it as data.** Stage one fills the computers' shares under a soft cap; stage two carves the human's exact share out by overwrite; a shuffle then places everything. The whole starting position is reproducible from five numbers (the level's share, the cap 15, the armies' option value, the centres' three slot positions, and the 46 of the board) plus the two draw shapes (capped seat fill, draw-and-remove shuffle). From Q-STA-01, Q-MAP-10, Q-STA-02, Q-STA-03.
- **The difficulty is a territory-share knob with an exact dial.** 5/9/14/16 of 46 is a spread of about a quarter of the board between the easiest and hardest settings — and nothing else changes. A malpaco difficulty control should say exactly what it moves. From Q-STA-04 and F016.
- **The starting armies are noise.** One to three armies per country cannot decide the opening; the opening is decided by where the deal put each seat's countries and where the three centres landed (the centres being F015's income events). A starting-armies option above 3 (registered-only here) would matter more. From Q-STA-02 and F013.
- **The first-seat quirk is a design decision to make, not copy.** Whether malpaco draws the first player plainly (one draw) or reproduces the original's animated selection with its decrement is a design question; the behaviour to imitate or drop is now known. From Q-STA-03.

## Alternatives considered

- **The cap might cap holdings, not draws.** Excluded: the counter increments before the cap test and counts unappended draws; a holding cap would test the appended count. The bytecode's increment-before-test order matches the text.
- **The human's share might exceed the minimum sometimes.** Excluded for this build: no path writes the human's number into the deal list except the overwrite (the fill's append gate names computer seats only). The all-human branch could, but is unreachable here.
- **The all-human minimum (11) might be a level-independent design constant** rather than an override. True as read: it replaces the level's value unconditionally in that branch. Its *why* is not decodable; its balance is out of scope (rule 19).
- **The first-seat decrement might be a decompiler misrender.** Possible but uncorrectable here: one decompiler, and the bytecode listing shows the same subtraction and wrap. Stated as read, with T28's check to settle the behaviour.
- **The deal's turn draw might exist for the generator's sake** (deliberate spacing of draws). Not decodable: the effect (one consumed value) is certain, the intent is not.
- **A registered multi-human deal (2 or 3 humans) might differ in kind.** The handler's structure (per-seat overwrite of *a* entries each) says how it would run, but the build cannot reach it and rule 19 keeps it out: noted, not answered.

## What this does not establish

- **That the decompiler is faithful in every branch.** One decompiler (ProjectorRays 0.2.0); the bytecode cross-check covers every constant and draw this finding names (19 checks), not every branch of the handlers; no run corroborates any behaviour.
- **The stage score.** The frame order around the starting chain — which frames carry the display script and the start-of-game script, and where the first turn's frame sits — is the score's, not decoded; only the call order inside the frame script is code.
- **The registered build,** and anything gated by the upgrade check (rule 19): the all-human deal's balance, the multi-human deals, the armies option's five values in play, the seat toggles. What registration changes here is `unknown`.
- **The statistical balance of the deal.** How near-even the computers' shares are, how often each seat goes first, how the armies distribute — measured properties, T28's; this finding states the mechanisms and the constants only. The corroboration plans count what play shows, conditioned on the draws; no gate's draw rate is read as a played frequency.
- **The random generator itself** — Director's runtime, not the Lingo (T34, Q-RND-02/Q-RND-03). "Uniform" is the function's documented range, nothing more.
- **The unset-minimum path** (levels 5 to 10 from a hand-edited defaults file): whether the runtime errors, treats the loop bound as zero, or something else is not decodable from the decompiled text.
- **DOS Isle Wars.** Nothing here is claimed for it (rule 19).
