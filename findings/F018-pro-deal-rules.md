## Answer

Every source below is `static:decompile` of `Islepro.exe`, sha256 `c4d7a248646454adeb48232e3ac7b3d2c0f08b290051a79d6fe6e47bb76b594e`; scripts are cited as in `questions/pro-static.md` (kind, number, name when it has one, handler). Nothing is quoted: the rules are described in our words (rule 18), and the deal's numbers are cited only as the checked output of `experiments/E016-pro-deal-reading/check_deal_constants.py` or as plain values (rule 20). "Seat" is 1 to 4; the opponent table's entry for a seat is 1 for a human and 0 for a computer. The deal runs over the fixed 46-country board of F017 (C-BRD-01, C-BRD-02); the per-country numbering below is that board's. A "draw of 1 to n" is how Director's random function is read; the generator itself is Director's runtime (Q-RND-02, T34) and no distribution is claimed beyond uniformity of the function's documented range.

**In one paragraph.** A fresh game runs a fixed chain: the new-game reset swaps every country's piece back to its empty-country image and empties the card hands, a display frame makes the board visible and announces the setup phase, and one frame script then does the whole starting state in three calls — the deal, the centre placement, the first-seat selection. The deal handler first zeroes the game-level state (the game-over, quit, fast-forward, scoring and turn-local globals), then fills a list to 46 entries by drawing seats and appending only computer seats' draws, under a soft per-seat threshold of 15 on a counter of first draws, applied as two sweeps of four ordered seat checks that can redraw up to eight times in one fill iteration; each human seat then overwrites exactly its level's number of entries (5, 9, 14 or 16; 11 in the registered all-human branch this build cannot reach); the list is shuffled by repeated draw-and-remove (one draw per removed entry, 46 in all); and a per-country pass builds the ownership table, the per-seat country lists, the display, and one army draw of 1 to the starting-armies option per country — an option forced to 3 in the unregistered build. The centres are three uniform distinct countries drawn from a full-board shuffle of the same shape. The first-seat selection is an animated draw of 9 to 18 iterations whose stored result is decremented by one seat at the end; the turn-advance step then increments that value before dispatch, so under the simpler reading the last-shown seat plays first, with the score's frame order the one piece not decoded. No file loads a prescribed ownership, army or centre position, and the only starting state a file can move is the human's exact dealt share (the difficulty, which the unregistered forcing does not replace). What varies between games is exactly the deal, the armies, the centres and the first seat — the board never does.

### The deal

#### Q-STA-01 How are the territories dealt to the seats?

- **As read (about 90%).** One handler does all of it, called once per game from the start-of-game frame script (the only caller, found by search). In order:
  - **The reset.** Before any draw the handler zeroes the game-level state: the quit-early flag, the fast-forward flag, the game counter, the stale board-click distance, the game-over flag, the action and clicked-country globals, and the do-not-score counter — which it sets to the number of human seats (F012's Q-SUR-05 reads that counter at the game's end; here is where it is born). It rebuilds the four-entry copy of the opponent table that play reads, empties the deal list, and moves the board marker off-screen.
  - **The fill loop.** While the deal list holds fewer than 46 entries: draw a seat (1 to 4); increment that seat's draw counter (unconditionally, before any test); apply the cap (two sweeps of four ordered seat checks, Q-MAP-10 below); and append the final value to the deal list only when it names a computer seat. The entry count advances only on an append, so the loop repeats — with draws on human seats wasted — until the list holds 46. In the unregistered build the opponent table is forced to one human at seat 1 (Q-AI-03), so only seats 2, 3 and 4 can be appended: the fill is a soft-threshold, uneven-in-time split of the 46 entries among the three computer seats.
  - **The all-human branch.** If all four seats are human, the fill loop is skipped (the entry count is pre-set to 46) and the list is instead filled with 46 uniform draws over all four seats, with no cap, and the minimum below becomes 11 regardless of the level. The unregistered build cannot reach this branch: the defaults forcing and the locked seat toggles (Q-AI-03) always leave exactly one human. It is stated here because the handler carries it, not analysed: its balance is a registered-build question (rule 19, out of scope).
  - **The minimum overwrite.** A single value is taken from the level: 5 at level 1, 9 at level 2, 14 at level 3, 16 at level 4 — no branch exists for 5 to 10 (Q-STA-04 below). Then each human seat, in seat order, overwrites the next entries of the deal list with its own number, that many per seat, one shared position counter walking the list. In this build's one-human game, exactly the first *a* entries of the list become the human seat's number.
  - **The shuffle.** The whole list is shuffled by repeated draw-and-remove: while entries remain, one is drawn uniformly from those remaining and moved to a new list. With 46 entries the loop runs 46 times, drawing bounds 46, 45, … 1, one entry removed per pass (Q-MAP-10's shuffle note). The human's overwritten entries therefore end at uniformly random positions of the board, as do the computers'.
  - **A turn draw that decides nothing.** The handler zeroes the turn global and immediately draws it fresh (1 to 4) — but nothing ever reads that value: the only caller runs the centre placement and then the first-seat selection next, and the selection overwrites the turn global before the game's first turn (Q-STA-03). The draw does advance the game's random generator, so the armies' particular values depend on it, but it decides nothing itself.
  - **The per-country pass.** The handler then walks the board's 46 countries in order. For each it reads the shuffled list's entry (the owner), appends the country to that seat's country list, updates the seat's on-screen country count, swaps the country's piece image for the owner's colour (the seat-to-colour mapping is a four-letter table built at movie start, `MovieScript 1 Startmovie` / `startMovie()`), waits a short fixed delay, and draws the country's starting armies (Q-STA-02), storing it in the army table and writing it to the country's on-board text field. When the pass ends, the ownership table, the army table and the four per-seat country lists are complete and the board is displayed: the deal is done. The deal list is the global `BOARDSET` (global slot 66) — it is stored and read through that global during the fill, the overwrite, the shuffle and the per-country pass — and no other script names it (found by search); its scope is a global, its only reader is this handler.
- **The exactness of the human's share (a sharpening of F016's C-AI-08).** In the unregistered build the human seat ends the deal with *exactly* its level's number of countries — not "at least" that number. No entry of the deal list can hold the human's number except through the overwrite: the fill appends computer seats only. F016's wording ("the seat ends with at least that many") is correct only for the all-human branch, where the pre-filled draws can land on any seat; that branch is unreachable here. The human's share in this build is a constant of the level, and only its *positions* are drawn.
- **Same rule?** No. Every seat's countries come from the same deal and the same shape of randomness, but the human's count is fixed by the level while the computers' counts come out of the soft-threshold draw-and-append; and only the computers' draws meet the threshold at all.

### The cap on territories per player

#### Q-MAP-10 (deal half) What caps the number of territories a player can be dealt, and what happens at the cap?

- **As read (the mechanism about 90%; the resulting distribution is not claimed).** There is no cap on *holdings*: the threshold — 15 — is tested against a per-seat counter of that seat's first draws, and the counter is not bounded by it. The fill keeps four counters; a first draw increments the drawn seat's counter before any test, unconditionally, and the increment continues past 15 (nothing clamps a counter or stops it incrementing). The threshold is then applied as **two sweeps of four ordered seat checks** (seats 1, 2, 3, 4, then 1, 2, 3, 4 again): each check tests the *current* drawn seat and, when that seat's counter exceeds 15, redraws it once. Because a redraw replaces the seat the later checks of the same sweep test, one fill iteration can redraw up to eight times, not twice, and the value can be re-tested within the sweep. Three consequences follow:
  - The counters overcount and are unbounded: a seat's counter grows for draws that land on the human seat (never appended in this build), for draws that are redrawn away, and for first draws that continue after it has passed 15 (a first draw onto a seat already counted 16 times raises its counter to 17 before the check). So a counter runs ahead of the seat's actual holdings and has no ceiling of 16.
  - An over-threshold seat can still receive an appended entry, because the last redraw of the second sweep is never re-tested: whatever seat it lands on is appended unchecked. (A second-sweep redraw to a lower-numbered seat, whose check in that sweep has already run, is likewise not re-tested before the append.) The two consecutive redraws need not both land on the same seat — the final redraw alone can put an over-threshold seat into the list. So the threshold is a strong bias, not a wall.
  - The fill appends 46 entries for computer seats (the loop stops only when the list holds 46); the human overwrite then replaces the first *a* of them with the human's seat number, so the computers end holding 46 − *a* (41, 37, 32 or 30 as the level takes 5, 9, 14 or 16 — arithmetic checked in the record). Those 46 holdings, not 46 − *a*, are what the counters counted (plus every redraw and every human-landing draw), so counters and holdings are different quantities. The resulting distribution of the computers' counts is **a hypothesis for T28, not claimed here**; in particular, near-even shares do not follow from the counter's behaviour.
  - No territory starts neutral: every one of the 46 entries names a seat after the fill (F017's board half of this question, confirmed at the deal level — the fill's loop bound and the per-country pass's bound are the same 46).
- **Same rule?** No. The human's share bypasses the threshold entirely (it is written by the overwrite, after the fill); only the computers' draws meet it.
- **Sources:** as Q-STA-01, with `MovieScript 5 Putinboard` / `Putinboard()` (the two sweeps of four threshold checks and the four counters) and `experiments/E016-pro-deal-reading/measurements/deal-constants-r2d.json` (the 8 comparisons at 15 in seat order 1,2,3,4,1,2,3,4, each followed by its own redraw draw that reassigns the tested seat; the four counters written before the first comparison; the shuffle's one-draw-per-removal loop; and the share arithmetic).
- **What would change it.** A reading of the threshold as a holding cap would change everything above — excluded by the counter's increment-before-test order, which the bytecode shows. A misrender of one sweep would leave 4 comparisons, not 8; the listing shows 8 in the two sweeps.
- **T28.** Over at least 20 starts (the question map's plan), record the three computer seats' country counts and whether any seat holds above 15. The reading's signature is the unchecked final redraw: an above-15 holding is possible without two redraws landing on the same seat, so T28 should treat such a holding as consistent with the reading, not as a contradiction. Whether the counts are near-even, and how often any exceeds 15, are what T28 measures (hypotheses, not predictions of this reading). Expect the human's count to sit exactly at the level's number every time.

### The starting armies

- **As read (about 90%).** One draw per country, and nothing else: inside the per-country pass, each of the 46 countries receives a fresh uniform draw of 1 to the starting-armies option, independent of every other country and of the owner; the value is stored in the army table and written to the country's on-board text field. In the unregistered build the starting-armies option is forced to 3 before any game runs (Q-AI-03), so **every country starts with 1 to 3 armies**, human- and computer-held alike. The option itself offers five values — 1, 3, 5, 7 and 10 — and is locked in the unregistered build: its handler refuses the click with the registered-only notice, like the seat toggles (`BehaviorScript 496 startingnumarmybehavior` / `mouseDown()`). The defaults file's seventh item carries the starting-armies value the game loads; its validation corrects only 0 to 3, but the unregistered forcing runs **after** the load and validation and replaces the value with 3 unconditionally (`MovieScript 458 getdefaults` / `Getdefaults()`), so no file-supplied armies value survives in this build — the hand-edited path claimed in round 1 is removed here; the value is not read again after the forcing. The options display's sprite mapping covers only the five standard values (`BehaviorScript 457` / `exitFrame()`; `BehaviorScript 479` / `exitFrame()`), but in this build the value is 3 regardless. The difficulty value enters the armies nowhere: the deal handler's only use of it is the minimum branch (Q-STA-04).
- **Same rule?** Yes — one rule for every country and every seat: 1 to the option's value, drawn independently. No seat term, no country term, no difficulty term.

### The setup after the deal

#### Q-STA-03 Does any setup step happen after the deal, and in what order?

A frame earlier in the chain makes the board visible and announces the setup phase (`BehaviorScript 2` / `exitFrame()`); the start-of-game frame clears the status lines before the deal and announces the centres phase between the deal and the placement. In order:
  - **The centre placement** (`MovieScript 51 putinproductioncenters` / `Putinproductioncenters()`). It builds a list of all 46 countries and shuffles it by the same repeated draw-and-remove as the deal; the entries at positions 12, 24 and 37 of the shuffled list become the three production centres — three uniform, distinct countries anywhere on the board, independent of ownership; each is then announced and coloured in turn. F015's Q-CEN-01 reading, re-derived here in full: the placement runs over the whole board after the deal, so a centre can land on any seat's country.
  - **The first-seat selection** (`MovieScript 399 WHOGOESFIRST` / `WHOGOESFIRST()`). It announces the selection, then draws an iteration count of 9 to 18 (a draw of 1 to 10 plus 8) and iterates: draw a seat (1 to 4), redrawing up to twice while the drawn seat repeats the previous iteration's seat; move the first-player marker to the drawn seat's row; sound the click; store the drawn seat as the previous; and pause — except after the last iteration. When the loop ends, the stored seat is **decremented by one, wrapping 1 back to 4**, so immediately after the selection the stored value is one seat below the marker's last shown position. That is not where it stays: the turn-start frame calls the advance handler, which increments the stored seat by one (wrapping 4 back to 1) before it dispatches play — F012 reads this as the deliberate compensation of the decrement (`MovieScript 10 Checkturn` / `CheckTurn()`; `BehaviorScript 14` / `exitFrame()`; F012's Q-TRN-02). Under the simpler reading the first turn lands on the seat the marker last showed. The score has not been decoded, so a path that dispatched play without the advance is not excluded, and F012 puts the first-turn reading at about 80%.
  - **The first turn.** After the selection the frame ends and the game's first turn begins through that advance (F012's turn-start frame). Nothing else runs between the deal and the first turn: the deal list is the global `BOARDSET` (slot 66), which no other script reads; the centres and the first seat are the only calls, and no other script calls any of the three (the centre placement has one other caller — the event frame, F015's — which is not in the starting chain).

## Observations

### The deal, step by step

| Step | Decides | Draws |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reset (before the deal) | the game-over, quit, fast-forward, scoring, action and clicked-country globals zeroed; the do-not-score counter set to the human-seat count; the opponent-table copy rebuilt; the marker parked | none |
| Fill loop | the 46 entries, computers only, under the soft threshold | a seat (1 to 4) per entry; up to eight redraws across two sweeps of four seat checks when the current drawn seat's counter exceeds 15 |
| All-human branch | registered-only: 46 uniform draws over all four seats, minimum 11 | 46 draws of 1 to 4 |
| Minimum | the human's exact share: 5/9/14/16 at levels 1/2/3/4 | none |
| Overwrite | each human seat's entries, in seat order | none |
| Shuffle | the entries' final board positions | a draw of the remaining list's size, 46 times (bounds 46 down to 1) |
| Turn draw | nothing (always overwritten by the first-seat selection) | one draw of 1 to 4 |
| Per-country pass | ownership, the per-seat lists, the display, the armies | one draw of 1 to the armies option, per country |
| Centres (after) | the three production centres | a full-board shuffle; the entries at its 12th, 24th and 37th positions |
| First seat (after) | the game's first player | 9 to 18 iterations of a seat draw with anti-repeat redraws, then a one-seat decrement with wrap; the turn advance's one-seat increment with wrap follows before play (F012) |

### The soft threshold's counter subtleties

The threshold is best read as three facts. First, the counters count *first draws*, not appends: draws onto the human seat and draws later redrawn away still count, so a seat's counter runs ahead of its actual holdings. Second, the counters are not bounded at 15 or 16: the increment is unconditional and happens before the test, and it keeps happening after the counter has passed the threshold (a first draw onto a seat counted 16 times makes 17). Third, the threshold is applied as two sweeps of four ordered checks (seat order 1,2,3,4,1,2,3,4); each check tests the current seat and its redraw reassigns the value the later checks of the same sweep test, so one fill iteration can redraw up to eight times, and the final redraw is never re-tested. An over-threshold seat can therefore be appended even when two consecutive redraws do not both land on it: the last redraw of the second sweep is unchecked, as is a second-sweep redraw to a seat whose check in that sweep already ran. The bytecode shows exactly eight comparisons at 15 in those two seat-order sweeps, each followed by its redraw draw and a reassignment of the tested seat, and the four counters written before the first comparison (`deal-constants-r2d.json`). Nothing in the handler caps the human's overwrite: the minimum is written after the fill and is not subject to the threshold.

### The exactness of the human's share

C-AI-08 (F016) states the level "guarantees each human seat a minimum of starting countries". In this build the guarantee is exact: the fill never appends the human's number, so the overwrite's *a* entries are the only entries the human can hold. The "at least" wording fits the registered all-human branch only (46 pre-filled draws can land anywhere, and there the minimum is 11 regardless of level). Malpaco should model the unregistered deal as an *exact* share. This is a proposed sharpening of C-AI-08, for promotion after review.

### The two dead and one live oddity of the starting chain

- The deal's turn draw is dead as a value — the first-seat selection overwrites the turn global before anything reads it — but it still consumes one random value, so the armies' particular draws depend on it having happened. A faithful re-implementation keeps the draw (or accepts different army values); a clean-room design would drop it.
- The first-seat selection's final decrement is cancelled by the turn-advance step's increment (F012): immediately after the selection the stored seat is one below the position the marker last showed, and the first turn then dispatches on the incremented value — the last-shown seat under the simpler reading. Whether that pair is deliberate or an off-by-one that happens to cancel is not decodable; the score is not decoded, so a turn path that skipped the advance is not excluded. T28's first-turn check (Q-STA-03) settles the behaviour; the design choice is malpaco's.
- The new-game reset writes one misspelled global name where its siblings are spelled correctly — the game-over flag's clear in that handler lands on a name nothing declares, so the flag is in fact cleared only by the deal that immediately follows (whose correct clear is part of Q-STA-01's reset list). This is consistent with F012's complete game-over-setter list, which does not include the reset handler. Harmless in the fresh-game flow, since no script reads the flag between the reset and the deal.

### What the unregistered build fixes before any deal

The defaults handler's unregistered forcing runs at every start, before the first game and after every options change: one human seat (seat 1), event frequency 2, starting armies 3. The seat toggles and the armies option refuse clicks with the registered-only notice; the level is not forced. So the deal this finding describes always runs with exactly one human, 1-to-3 armies, and the level's exact share for that human — the options around it are fixed furniture in this build.

## Inferences

- **The deal is a two-stage draw, and malpaco can take it as data.** Stage one fills 46 entries for the computers under a soft threshold on a first-draw counter; stage two overwrites the human's exact share into the first *a* of them; a shuffle then places everything. The whole starting position is reproducible from five numbers (the level's share, the threshold 15, the armies' option value, the centres' three slot positions, and the 46 of the board) plus the two draw shapes (soft-threshold seat fill, draw-and-remove shuffle). From Q-STA-01, Q-MAP-10, Q-STA-02, Q-STA-03.
- **The difficulty is a territory-share knob with an exact dial.** 5/9/14/16 of 46 is a spread of about a quarter of the board between the easiest and hardest settings — and nothing else changes. A malpaco difficulty control should say exactly what it moves. From Q-STA-04 and F016.
- **What the starting armies do to the opening is not established here, and is left as a question, not a conclusion.** The deal gives every country an independent 1-to-3 draw (Q-STA-02), but whether that decides anything is an opening-analysis question this static reading does not answer, and the registered armies options are out of scope (rule 19). It is an unestablished question for later research.
- **The first-seat quirk is a design decision to make, not copy.** Whether malpaco draws the first player plainly (one draw) or reproduces the original's animated selection with its decrement-then-advance pair is a design question; the behaviour to imitate or drop is now known. From Q-STA-03.

## Alternatives considered

- **The threshold might cap holdings, not first draws.** Excluded: the counter increments before the test and counts unappended draws; a holding cap would test the appended count. The bytecode's increment-before-test order matches the text, and the four counters are written before the first comparison.
- **The human's share might exceed the minimum sometimes.** Excluded for this build: no path writes the human's number into the deal list except the overwrite (the fill's append gate names computer seats only). The all-human branch could, but is unreachable here.
- **The all-human minimum (11) might be a level-independent design constant** rather than an override. True as read: it replaces the level's value unconditionally in that branch. Its *why* is not decodable; its balance is out of scope (rule 19).
- **The first-seat decrement might be uncancelled, so the seat before the shown one plays first.** F012 does not exclude it (the score is not decoded); it reads the advance step as incrementing the stored seat, which cancels the decrement, and calls that the simpler reading at about 80%. F018 states the pair as read and leaves the first-turn result to T28, not asserted.
- **The deal's turn draw might exist for the generator's sake** (deliberate spacing of draws). Not decodable: the effect (one consumed value) is certain, the intent is not.
- **A registered multi-human deal (2 or 3 humans) might differ in kind.** The handler's structure (per-seat overwrite of *a* entries each) says how it would run, but the build cannot reach it and rule 19 keeps it out: noted, not answered.

## What this does not establish

- **That the decompiler is faithful in every branch.** One decompiler (ProjectorRays 0.2.0); the bytecode cross-check covers every constant, draw and loop structure this finding names (25 checks), not every branch of the handlers; no run corroborates any behaviour. It also cannot execute: the up-to-eight-redraws bound and the 46 shuffle draws are counted by hand from the loop structures the checker records, not run.
- **The stage score.** The frame order around the starting chain — which frames carry the display script and the start-of-game script, and where the first turn's frame sits — is the score's, not decoded; only the call order inside the frame script is code.
- **The registered build,** and anything gated by the upgrade check (rule 19): the all-human deal's balance, the multi-human deals, the armies option's five values in play, the seat toggles. What registration changes here is `unknown`.
- **The statistical balance of the deal.** How near-even the computers' shares are, how the counters drift, how often any seat exceeds 15, how often each seat goes first, how the armies distribute — measured properties, T28's; this finding states the mechanisms and the constants only. The corroboration plans count what play shows, conditioned on the draws; no gate's draw rate is read as a played frequency.
- **The random generator itself** — Director's runtime, not the Lingo (T34, Q-RND-02/Q-RND-03). "Uniform" is the function's documented range, nothing more.
- **The unset-minimum path** (levels 5 to 10 from a hand-edited defaults file): whether the runtime errors, treats the loop bound as zero, or something else is not decodable from the decompiled text.
- **DOS Isle Wars.** Nothing here is claimed for it (rule 19).
